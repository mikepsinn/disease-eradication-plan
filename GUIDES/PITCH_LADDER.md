# EOS Pitch Ladder

Voice per [STYLE_GUIDE.md](STYLE_GUIDE.md). The register is the **1950s "World of Tomorrow" advertisement**: a cheerful narrator selling you the gleaming future, the same voice as the [educational film](../knowledge/educational-film.qmd) and the [EOS brochure](../knowledge/solution/earth-optimization-services.qmd). Each tier is the one before it with more proof bolted on.

**The rules that govern all four:**

- **Sell the future, not the fear.** Lead with the Civilization of Tomorrow (less war, fewer funerals, more money), not "you're going to die." Aspiration is the melody.
- **Stakes ride in on a smile.** Deliver suffering as chipper ad understatement: "Say goodbye to the heartbreak of watching your father forget your name!" The gap between the cheerful tone and the devastating content is the comedy. Never melodrama.
- **The salesman never flinches.** State the horrifying thing in the same bright voice you'd use for a new refrigerator. The deadpan is the whole trick (if the narrator sounds scared or self-aware, the gap closes and it dies).
- **A company, not a movement.** You're a Humanity Manager (owner-operator) who already owns a share, not a recruit being asked to believe.
- **The mechanism gets a wink, not a lecture.** "We buy up the old war business and gently point it at a better line of work." Don't say "weapons makers," "redirect lobbying budgets," or anything that sounds like a consulting deck.
- **"Spend it curing disease," not "give it back."** The money is spent on the thing no family can buy alone.
- **You decide where it goes (the trust layer).** Allocated by every human, one vote each, not for sale. Disease is simply what humanity votes for first. Use this to answer "who decides / why trust you," not as the hook.
- **Jokes stay short** (5 to 15 words) and the parenthetical undercut is the secret weapon.

## 10 seconds (the hook)

Pick by audience. One breath.

- **Heartbreak (default for the public):** "Say goodbye to the heartbreak of suffering and dying slowly! Earth Optimization Services brings the Civilization of Tomorrow straight to your door: fewer wars, fewer boring funerals, and lots and lots of money. Also, you own the company." (Social/web variant: "fewer boring funerals 🥱". In print, let "boring" carry it. Strong alternate button: "Operators are standing by. The operator is you.")
- **Gee-whiz:** "Friends! Are you and everyone you love still dying of disease? In the Civilization of Tomorrow, that is a thing of the past, and you already own a share of the company that builds it."
- **Power transfer (anti-government / jaded):** "Tired of your government spending your money frightening strangers instead of keeping your family alive? Earth Optimization Services buys it out and hands the decision back to you. The future: now under new management."
- **Carnival bark:** "Friends! The war machine is sold to anyone with money! Congratulations: you're anyone!"
- **Product-claim button:** "Now with lobbyists who work for you. They're confused, but the checks clear."

## 30 seconds (the mechanism, with a wink)

> Here at Earth Optimization Services, we believe every family deserves a future, and we have found a way to deliver one. We buy the companies that own your government, one share at a time, and gently point them at a more profitable line of work than manufacturing orphans: curing disease. The cost is one penny of every government dollar now spent on explosions, about $27 billion a year, enough to test every promising medicine instead of almost none. The result is a healthier, wealthier tomorrow, brought to you by a company you happen to own. Your vote is free. The future asks so little: a penny, and thirty seconds of your attention.

## 5 minutes (the thesis, still smiling)

Open in register, then walk the six beats without dropping the voice:

> Welcome to the Civilization of Tomorrow. Mind the wires. Your species has built enough nuclear weapons to end the world about 122 times. The world can end once. You did the other 121 for practice. We find this charming, the way one finds a toddler charming for trying to eat a doorknob. The good news, friends, is that it is entirely fixable, and the fix fits in your pocket.

1. **The problem, stated cheerfully.** Six hundred dollars on weapons for every one dollar spent figuring out why your citizens drop dead. The strange part is that nobody is even winning: the people who sell the weapons would be richer in a peaceful, healthy, far larger economy. This is not greed beating generosity; it is everyone, arms dealers included, failing to notice they would all make more money if fewer people were dead.
2. **The fix fits in your pocket.** No new money. Move one penny of every dollar now spent on explosions, about $27 billion a year, everyone trimming the same penny so nobody's army ends up smaller than anyone else's. Spend a billion once; collect twenty-seven billion a year, forever. The future, on layaway.
3. **How the company does it.** We buy the companies that own your government, one share at a time, and ask the board, politely, to point its lobbyists at something nicer than manufacturing orphans. Shares come with votes, votes come with board seats, and all of it is perfectly legal. The owners get richer on the way in. Then the money runs through a machine where the public decides four-fifths of it and nobody gets to skim, a thing your planet currently files under science fiction.
4. **What it pays.** A floor (if the whole thing flops, you are holding a fine, fee-free basket of stocks), a Light model (the treaty alone, the economy about 4x larger in fifteen years), and a Deluxe model (the works, nearer 27x). Your Mr. Buffett got rich riding a machine that robs the room every few years; this one repairs the room, so everything else you own rides along.
5. **Why you can trust the salesman.** You own as much of it as the people who run it do, which your species finds more suspicious than if they had stolen everything. Money and influence sit at separate counters by design: investors get money and no control, every human gets one equal vote, and the vote is not for sale. The counter between them is the product.
6. **How to order.** You are already a Humanity Manager. Two things on your queue: cast your vote (free, thirty seconds) and bring two more Managers on board. Want the money too? Buy in from about $200. The vote is yours either way.

## 15 minutes (the full showroom tour)

Everything above, expanded, plus the fine print a careful customer wants. Keep the brochure voice; let the dark facts land deadpan.

- **The structure.** A company over four legally-separate departments (a charity, a political arm, a for-profit, a foundation), because one outfit legally cannot take donations *and* lobby *and* sell shares. The walls between them are what keep it honest and un-skimmable.
- **What you actually buy.** Equity in the for-profit core (money in, more money out, no controls). Not the vote, which every human already holds. The deposit-based Prize and the bonds are separate optional models on the lot.
- **Three engines, in case one stalls (a hedge).** The market engine (buy the companies, point the lobbying somewhere kinder); the political engine (Incentive Alignment Bonds, which make voting yes pay better than voting no, corruption capped at 20% and shown on the dashboard); and the coordination engine (the Earth Optimization Prize, a pool that pays out for hitting the health-and-wealth targets, where you earn your share by recruiting and cannot buy it).
- **The money, honestly.** Best to enter before the good news is priced in. The floor is dull. The upside is lumpy and depends on the treaty actually passing, which is the hard part. The bond's eyebrow-raising 272% is not a market return; it is a political-risk premium, one percent of $27 billion measured against a $1 billion campaign.
- **The objections, answered without dropping the smile.** Won't peace ruin defense stocks? You hold a basket, you caught the buy-up wave, and the pivot is to a larger economy, not to zero. Isn't this a security? Yes; the shares and the deposits are securities, sold as securities, not dressed up as "utility tokens." What stops a crook? No chief executive to bribe, four-fifths allocated by the crowd, every dollar on a public ledger.
- **The closing.** If it works, you do not merely own a tidy fund. Every human is a Humanity Manager with one vote in how the planet is run, the government's worst departments are quietly replaced by software that accepts no campaign donations, and "disease" becomes a thing your grandchildren study in history class, between "smallpox" and "kings."

## Audience variants

- **Public / individual:** heartbreak hook (the family, the funerals), suffering as chipper understatement.
- **Anti-government / libertarian:** power-transfer hook ("now under new management," "the decision back to you"). To this crowd "fund disease research" sounds like more government; "you decide, not them" lands better.
- **Institutions / investors:** open with the showroom fine print (nobody who runs it owns a share, money and influence at separate counters, the floor, the four departments). Keep it bright but lead with structure.
- **Everyone, eventually:** sequence them. Sell the future first; show the wiring last.
