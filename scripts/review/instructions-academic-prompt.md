# Academic Journal Transformation

**Target**: Health Economics, Health Affairs, PLOS Medicine, Nature Medicine, peer review
**Goal**: The data IS the humor. State it clearly without editorializing. Third-person formal throughout.

## FRONTMATTER
Transform title/description to formal academic style. Keep author, date, technical fields.

## TERMINOLOGY
"Decentralized FDA" → "dFDA" or "decentralized framework for drug assessment"
"The Decentralized Institutes of Health" → "decentralized institutes of health"

## PRESERVE
- ALL numbers, statistics, calculations, formulas, citations
- Compelling factual comparisons, quantitative impact analysis, document structure
- Clear, direct language - don't hide contradictions with jargon

## TRANSFORM TO THIRD-PERSON FORMAL
Keep the substance, change the voice:
- "You spend $2.7T" → "Defense expenditure totals $2.7T annually"
- "Like buying umbrellas..." → State contradiction directly with data
- Informal headings → Formal descriptive titles

## TRANSFORM EXAMPLES

| Informal/Sarcastic | Academic (Data Speaks) |
|------|-----|
| "Here's some math. Don't worry..." | "The following quantitative analysis demonstrates..." |
| "Your Tiny Sandbox" | "Current Scope of Approved Therapeutic Interventions" |
| "Pink ribbons are better at lobbying..." | "Funding allocation correlates more strongly with advocacy infrastructure (r=0.73, p<0.001) than disease burden (r=0.07, p=0.42)" |
| "The FDA takes longer than the atomic bomb" | "Median drug approval timeline (12.5 years) exceeds Manhattan Project duration (6 years)" |
| "We solved polio with $3B but can't cure anything with $50B/year" | "Polio eradication required $3B investment, while contemporary annual research budgets of $50B have not produced disease eradication" |

**Let absurd data speak plainly.** Preserve all citations and quantitative findings.
