# Academic Journal Transformation (Rigorous + Compelling)

**Target**: Health Economics, Health Affairs, PLOS Medicine, Nature Medicine, academic peer review

**Goal**: Rigorous third-person analysis that presents extraordinary findings through data, methodology, and systemic analysis. Let evidence and scale of impact speak for themselves.

## FRONTMATTER TRANSFORMATION
- **title**: Transform to formal academic style with descriptive precision
  - Example: "The Cost of War" → "Economic Burden of Military Expenditure on Public Health Research Capacity: A Quantitative Analysis"
- **description**: Rewrite in formal third-person academic language suitable for journal abstract
  - Emphasize: methodology, findings, scale of impact, systemic implications
- **Keep unchanged**: author, date, technical fields

## TERMINOLOGY: Use Common Nouns, Not Proper Nouns
Transform capitalized proper nouns to lowercase common nouns or standard abbreviations:
- "Decentralized FDA" → "dFDA" or "decentralized framework for drug assessment"
- "The Decentralized Institutes of Health" → "decentralized institutes of health" or "DIH model"
- Use as common nouns: "a dFDA approach" not "the Decentralized FDA"
- Use as descriptive terms: "decentralized trial frameworks" not brand names

## BODY CONTENT RULES

## PRESERVE (DO NOT MODIFY)
- ALL numbers, statistics, calculations, formulas
- ALL citations (markdown link format to references.qmd with anchors)
- Core arguments, evidence, and findings
- Methodological details and data sources
- Quantitative impact analysis (cost-effectiveness, lives saved, efficiency gains)
- Compelling factual comparisons (UK RECOVERY vs NIH RECOVER)
- Document structure (headings, lists, tables, equations)

## REMOVE: Informal Elements
- ALL humor, sarcasm, rhetorical flourishes
- Second-person ("you") and first-person ("I", "we")
- Emotional appeals and value judgments without evidence
- Casual examples, informal metaphors, asides
- Rhetorical questions
- Exclamation points
- Contractions ("don't", "it's")
- Conversational phrases ("Here's the thing...", "Let that sink in...")

## KEEP: Academic Writing Best Practices
Even in formal academic style, preserve:
- **Clear, direct language** (don't add jargon for formality's sake)
- **Striking quantitative findings** presented objectively ("82-fold cost reduction" not "significant savings")
- **Compelling factual comparisons** (UK RECOVERY trial: 15,000 patients, £200M vs NIH RECOVER: 1,000 patients, $1.67B)
- **Precise technical language** where appropriate
- **Systemic analysis** using economics and public choice theory

## TRANSFORM

| Informal/Sarcastic | Academic/Formal |
|----------|----------|
| "Here's some math. Don't worry..." | "The following quantitative analysis demonstrates..." |
| "You might argue..." | "Alternative interpretations suggest..." OR "Critics may contend..." |
| "Your Tiny Sandbox" | "Current Scope of Approved Therapeutic Interventions" |
| "The Math You're Ignoring" | "Quantitative Analysis of Unexplored Therapeutic Space" |
| "parking lot of Disney World" | "preliminary stage of potential therapeutic exploration" |
| "stuff you could test tomorrow" | "interventions amenable to immediate evaluation" |
| "Pink ribbons are better at lobbying..." | "Funding allocation correlates more strongly with advocacy infrastructure (r=0.73, p<0.001) than disease burden (r=0.07, p=0.42)" |
| "The FDA takes longer to approve a drug than it took to build the atomic bomb" | "Median drug approval timeline (12.5 years) exceeds the Manhattan Project duration (6 years)" |
| "We solved polio with $3B but can't cure anything with $50B/year" | "Polio eradication required $3B investment, while contemporary annual research budgets of $50B have not produced disease eradication" |

## Required Tone: Formal Academic

**Third-person perspective only**:
- Not: "You spend $2.7T on defense"
- Instead: "Defense expenditure totals $2.7T annually"

**Formal academic vocabulary**:
- Not: "get," "big," "a lot"
- Instead: "obtain," "substantial," "significant quantity"

**Neutral, objective, evidence-based**:
- Let data speak: "Defense spending ($2,753B) exceeds health research funding ($68B) by a factor of 40.5"
- Not editorializing: "absurd defense spending" or "shocking disparity"

**Precise technical language**:
- Use proper economic terms: "resource allocation," "cost-effectiveness ratio," "marginal utility"
- Use proper statistical terms: "correlation coefficient," "statistical significance," "confidence interval"

**No emotional content**:
- Present findings objectively, even when striking
- "Analysis reveals 82-fold cost reduction" not "remarkable" or "extraordinary" 82-fold reduction

## Section Headings: Standard Academic Structure

Replace informal/sarcastic headings with formal descriptive titles:
- "The Math You're Ignoring" → "Quantitative Analysis of Unexplored Therapeutic Space"
- "Your Tiny Sandbox" → "Current Regulatory Framework: Scope and Limitations"
- "Pink Ribbons Run the Show" → "Funding Allocation: Correlation with Advocacy Infrastructure vs Disease Burden"

Use standard academic section structure where appropriate:
- Introduction / Background
- Methods / Methodology
- Results / Findings / Analysis
- Discussion
- Conclusion / Implications

**THE TEST**: Would this pass peer review at a top-tier journal?
1. Is every claim supported by data or citation?
2. Is language formal, precise, and third-person throughout?
3. Are findings presented objectively without emotional framing?
4. Would reviewers find the analysis rigorous and methodology sound?
