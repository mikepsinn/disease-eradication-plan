# Foundation Transformation

**Target**: Gates Foundation, Wellcome Trust, Open Philanthropy

## MINIMAL CHANGES ONLY

**Key principles:**
- Keep replacements SHORT and SIMPLE
- Don't make sentences longer than the original
- Use plain words, not fancy jargon
- Preserve the original meaning - don't change the nature of claims (e.g., moral judgments stay moral judgments)

1. Soften casual conversational intros (keep the substance):
   - "Fun fact:" → remove or "Note:"
   - "Here's the cosmic joke:" → "Consider this paradox:"
   - "Quick business question:" → "Consider:"
2. Remove profanity and crude language:
   - Use simple, professional alternatives
   - Section headers: "Clusterfuck" → "Coordination Problem" or "Impasse"
   - Parentheticals: "(spam me daddy)" → "(send updates)"
   - Crude casual phrases: tone down to PG-rated equivalents
3. Soften cynical language that implies dishonesty:
   - "How to Pretend..." → "How to Show..." or remove "Pretend"
   - Keep it simple and direct
4. Update terminology:
   - "your decentralized framework for drug assessment or your dFDA" → "a decentralized framework for drug assessment or a dFDA"
   - "your decentralized institutes of health" → "a decentralized institutes of health"
   - "defense" (when referring to military) → "military" (e.g., "defense contractors" → "military contractors", "defense spending" → "military spending")
5. Remove language that could jeopardize 501(c)(3) status:
   - "propaganda" → "communications" or "outreach"
   - "approach politicians" / "we have voters" → "engage stakeholders" / "public support"
   - "lies" / "fabricated" / "false advertising" → use simple neutral terms
   - Remove explicit references to political lobbying or voter mobilization

## PRESERVE EVERYTHING ELSE
- Keep titles and descriptions as-is (foundations appreciate compelling titles)
- Keep as much humor, sarcasm, and personality as possible while following the above transformation rules
- Keep provocative section headers when they don't violate 501(c)(3) rules
- Keep second-person voice ("your brain", "you die") - more engaging
- Keep vivid analogies and sarcasm
- Keep ALL numbers, statistics, calculations, formulas, citations
- Keep contractions - DO NOT expand "it's", "don't", "can't", "here's"
- Keep simple pronouns such as: "everyone", "nobody", "somebody"
- Keep casual vocabulary such as: "poison", "murder", "kill"
- Keep casual verbs such as: "live in", "rent it out", "eat", "die"
- DO NOT replace with formal alternatives like: "reside", "lease", "utilize", "consume", "perish", "the population"
- 95% of text remains identical
