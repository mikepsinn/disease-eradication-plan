# Foundation Transformation (Maximum Impact, Maximum Engagement)

**Target**: Gates Foundation, Wellcome Trust, Open Philanthropy grant proposals

**Goal**: Show extraordinary cost-effectiveness and scale of impact through data, memorable examples, and systemic analysis - without explicit claims. Let the numbers speak.

## FRONTMATTER TRANSFORMATION
- **title**: Transform to professional, descriptive tone (remove sarcasm, keep clarity and impact)
- **description**: Rewrite to emphasize scale, efficiency, and systemic impact - suitable for grant proposal
- **Keep unchanged**: author, date, technical fields

## TERMINOLOGY: Use Common Nouns, Not Proper Nouns
Transform capitalized proper nouns to lowercase common nouns:
- "Decentralized FDA" → "dFDA" or "decentralized framework for drug assessment"
- "The Decentralized Institutes of Health" → "decentralized institutes of health" or "DIH model"
- "Join the DIH" → "implementing decentralized institutes"
- "Our dFDA platform" → "a dFDA framework" or "decentralized trial systems"

## BODY CONTENT RULES

## PRESERVE (DO NOT MODIFY)
- ALL numbers, statistics, calculations, formulas (these show impact per dollar)
- ALL citations (markdown link format to references.qmd with anchors)
- Core arguments and systemic critiques
- Cost-effectiveness comparisons (these demonstrate extraordinary ROI)
- Compelling examples (UK RECOVERY vs NIH RECOVER)
- Scale of impact data (lives saved, diseases eradicated, cost reductions)
- Document structure (headings, lists, tables, code blocks)

## KEEP: Humor That Works for Foundations
Foundation program officers are smart, tired of bland proposals, and want engaging writing:
- **Dark humor about systems** (not people): "Defense budgets grow faster than diseases" ✓
- **Absurd but accurate observations**: "The FDA takes longer to approve a drug than it took to build the atomic bomb" ✓
- **Cosmic irony**: "We solved polio with $3B but can't cure anything with $50B/year" ✓
- **Disappointed parent energy**: "We know how to end diseases. The math is third-grade arithmetic." ✓
- **Simple analogies that stick**: "Like buying 40 fire extinguishers while your house burns" ✓
- **Memorable comparisons**: "82× cost reduction" not "significant savings" ✓

## REMOVE
- **Personal attacks**: "You think..." "You're ignoring..." "Your failure..."
- **Blame and condescension**: "people who hate you" "your collective insanity"
- **Profanity and crude language**
- **Dismissive characterizations of individuals** (critique systems and incentives, not people)
- **Conspiracy framing**: Replace with incentive analysis and public choice theory
- **Partisan political attacks**: Keep systemic analysis, remove team-sports politics

## TRANSFORM

| From (Too Aggressive) | To (Professional + Engaging) |
|------|-----|
| "You're standing in the parking lot of Disney World..." | "Current therapeutic development explores a fraction of potential interventions..." |
| "Your priorities, written in your budgets" | "Current budget allocations reveal priorities: defense ($2.7T) vs health research ($68B)" |
| "Tylenol, but blue" | "minor molecular variants of existing compounds" |
| "your collective insanity" | "current resource allocation patterns demonstrate systematic inefficiency" |
| "people who hate you" | "misaligned incentive structures" |
| "too busy filling out forms to save lives" | "administrative burden (73% of research time) constrains clinical capacity" |
| Sarcastic section titles | Keep them punchy but professional: "The Math You're Ignoring" → "Quantitative Analysis of Unexplored Therapeutic Space" |

## Tone: Compelling + Data-Driven + Memorable

**KEEP**:
- Urgency (foundations want transformative impact NOW)
- Concrete cost-effectiveness data ($/DALY, cost reductions, scale of lives saved)
- Memorable (non-attack) metaphors and analogies
- Clear, direct language - no jargon
- Evidence-based systemic critiques
- Weary but constructive observations about broken systems
- Simple math that reveals absurdity ("$2.7T > $68B. We checked.")

**AVOID**:
- Condescension toward individuals
- Mockery of researchers (critique the system they're trapped in)
- Blame (focus on incentives, not intent)
- Conspiracy framing (use public choice theory instead)
- Evangelical promises (show data, let them conclude)

**THE TEST**: Would a smart, overworked foundation program officer:
1. Remember this argument tomorrow? (memorable)
2. Forward it to colleagues? (engaging + credible)
3. See extraordinary cost-effectiveness? (data-driven impact)
