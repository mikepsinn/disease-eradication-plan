# Prize Contributor ROI

## The Short Version

Contributing to the prize has no financial downside and an expected value of $149K–$521K in additional lifetime income per person, depending on how much of the plan gets implemented. The failure case pays ~108% return over 15 years. The success case multiplies your lifetime earnings by 13.5x–45x. There is no other investment on Earth with this structure.

---

## The Three Scenarios

### Scenario 1: The Prize Never Reaches Its Threshold
The assurance contract activates. You get your full principal back plus all accrued yield from the treasury's T-bill holdings. At a 5% annualized return over 15 years, that's **$1 in → $2.08 out**. Your downside is: you earned T-bill returns instead of whatever else you would have done with the money.

### Scenario 2: The Prize Funds the Plan, But Nothing Verified Works in 15 Years
Optimitron runs its global snapshot on the 15-year anniversary. Zero cumulative gains across all adopting jurisdictions. The global failure refund clause activates. Same result as Scenario 1: **full principal + 15 years of accrued yield returned.** You still get $2.08 per $1 contributed.

### Scenario 3: The Plan Works
This is where the math gets uncomfortable.

## What "Works" Means Financially

The model runs three trajectories. Here's what each one means for a person alive today, measured in cumulative lifetime income over an average remaining lifespan of 48.5 years:

| Trajectory | Year-20 avg income | Lifetime cumulative income | vs. current |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Current trajectory** (2.5% growth, no reform) | $20,500 | $1.18M | baseline |
| **Treaty trajectory** (1% reallocation + dFDA) | $339K | $16.1M | **+$14.9M / 13.5×** |
| **Wishonia trajectory** (full reform, dysfunction tax eliminated) | $1.16M | $53.3M | **+$52.1M / 45×** |

These are not projections of what will happen. They are the model's output for what happens if the mechanism works, based on post-WWII precedent (87.6% military spending cut → greatest economic boom in recorded history), disease burden elimination, and compounding growth over a lifetime. The 95% confidence intervals are wide: Treaty lifetime gain is $3.6M–$67.9M; Wishonia is $15.3M–$316M. Even the low end is large.

### Why the Numbers Are This Big

Three compounding effects:

**1. Military spending reallocation.** The 1% Treaty redirects ~$27B/year into clinical trials. This isn't a donation — it's capital moving from a sector with negative economic multipliers (weapons that destroy productive capacity) to a sector with the highest known multiplier (medical research). Post-WWII: the US cut military spending 87.6% in two years. What followed was the longest sustained economic expansion in American history.

**2. Political dysfunction tax elimination.** Current political dysfunction costs $101T/year globally — $12,600 per person annually. That's the opportunity cost of misallocated capital: $34T in foregone health outcomes, $4T in foregone scientific progress, $6T in foregone leadership capacity, $57T in foregone migration optimization. Eliminating even half of this unlocks a compound growth dividend that dwarfs the initial cost.

**3. The parasitic economy clock.** At current growth rates, the destructive economy (military + cybercrime) reaches 25% of global GDP in **8 years** and 50% in **15 years**. When extraction exceeds production, economies don't slow — they collapse. The current trajectory already prices in a ~10% probability of this outcome. The treaty doesn't just add income; it buys out of the collapse tail. The option value of avoiding parasitic-economy collapse is not in the $14.9M figure above — that number assumes the current trajectory holds. The real comparison is Treaty trajectory vs. collapse, which is $14.9M+ vs. potentially negative lifetime wealth.

## ROI for a Prize Contributor

### Example: $10,000 contribution

**Failure case (both branches):**
- Return: $10,000 × (1.05)^15 = **$20,790**
- ROI: +108% over 15 years, +5% annually
- Downside: opportunity cost vs. other investments only

**Success case (Treaty trajectory, p = 1% conservative):**
- Personal lifetime income gain: +$14.9M (average person globally)
- Expected value of that gain: 1% × $14,900,000 = **$149,000**
- Total EV including failure branch: $149,000 + (99% × $20,790) = **$169,582**
- On a $10,000 contribution: **expected ROI ≈ 1,596%**

**Success case (Wishonia trajectory, p = 1%):**
- Personal lifetime income gain: +$52.1M
- Expected value: 1% × $52,100,000 = **$521,000**
- Total EV: $521,000 + (99% × $20,790) = **$541,582**
- On a $10,000 contribution: **expected ROI ≈ 5,316%**

### At any contribution size

The lifetime income gain scales independently of contribution size — it accrues to you as a citizen of a world where the mechanism worked, not as a prize winner. Your $10 contribution gives you the same $14.9M lifetime income gain as a $10M contribution, because the gain is societal. What your contribution size determines is:

1. Whether the prize threshold gets hit (collective action problem — everyone needs to contribute enough)
2. How much you personally recover in the yield-bearing treasury if it fails

This means smaller contributions have *higher personal ROI* than larger ones on a per-dollar basis, which is unusual for any investment and is a direct consequence of the public-goods structure. The rational play for a selfish actor: contribute the minimum that counts as meaningful participation, collect T-bill returns on failure, and collect disproportionate lifetime income gains on success.

## The Honest Caveat

The $14.9M–$52.1M lifetime income gain is the model's output given its assumptions. The assumptions are:

- The 1% Treaty actually gets implemented and sustained
- The dFDA actually accelerates drug development as modeled (12.3× capacity increase)
- Post-WWII growth patterns recur when military spending is redirected
- The political dysfunction tax can actually be partially recovered through better governance
- Your lifespan is roughly average (48.5 remaining years)

If any of these assumptions are wrong, the upside is smaller. The downside (T-bill returns) doesn't change. This is the correct risk profile for a public-good investment: bounded downside, unbounded upside, transparent model.

The model is at manual.warondisease.org. Every number has a citation or a derivation. If you find a flaw, submit it as a challenge to the prize — that's the mechanism. Better model → better plan → better outcome. The prize funds whichever survives adversarial review.
