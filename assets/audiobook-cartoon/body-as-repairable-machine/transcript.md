**[00:04:34]** Every day, your body runs a maintenance routine that would make a German car engineer weep with joy and then immediately resign out of inadequacy:

**[00:07:06]** Your blood cell can become a brain cell.

**[00:07:10]** Any cell can become any other cell.

**[00:07:13]** It's like discovering every part of your car can transform into any other part. Need a new transmission? Just reprogram your floor mat. Need a new engine? Your cup holder has been an engine this entire time. It just forgot.

**[00:07:27]** You won a Nobel Prize for proving that your cells have no idea what they are and can be talked into being something else. This is the most important discovery in the history of medicine. You celebrated by going back to mostly not using it.

**[00:07:44]** Exhibit C: You're Debugging Your Code.

**[00:07:48]** Gene therapy is literally finding typos in your D.N.A. and correcting them:

**[00:07:54]** Luxturna (twenty seventeen): Fixes blindness caused by a mutated retinal pigment epithelium sixty-five, or R.P.E. sixty-five gene. One injection. Sight restored. The entire disease was a typo. One letter wrong, and you couldn't see. They fixed the letter.

**[00:08:15]** Zolgensma (twenty nineteen): Fixes spinal muscular atrophy. Babies who would die before age two now walk. The disease was a spelling error in one gene. You corrected the spelling. The baby walks. That's the whole story.

**[00:08:33]** Chimeric antigen receptor T-cell therapy, or CAR-T therapy (twenty seventeen): Reprograms your immune system to hunt cancer. Complete remission in "incurable" cases. You taught your white blood cells to be assassins. They were already assassins, technically, but now they're assassins with a target.

**[00:08:55]** You're not treating symptoms anymore. You're fixing the actual code. That's like going from "the car makes a weird noise, turn up the radio" to "the car makes a weird noise, let me look at the engine." It only took you two hundred thousand years to try the second approach.

**[00:09:12]** The Car Restoration Analogy.

**[00:09:14]** Think of your body as a nineteen eighty-seven Honda Civic with three point eight billion miles on it. You already know how to fix most of the parts:

**[00:09:24]** Joints: Like worn brake pads. You pop them out and put new ones in. You've been doing this since the nineteen sixties. It's boring now. Boring is good. Boring means you solved it.

**[00:09:38]** Arteries: Like clogged fuel lines. You shove a tiny metal tube (a "stent") in there and call it fixed. This works surprisingly well for something that sounds like plumbing.

**[00:09:50]** Hearts: Like a dead battery. You take one from another car (a car that no longer needs it, for reasons) and put it in yours. This is called a "transplant," which is a polite word for organ recycling.

**[00:10:03]** You're making progress on the harder stuff, too. Cancer is frame damage, and immunotherapy is your new welding torch. Alzheimer's is a bad transmission, and you're finally starting to understand the gears.

**[00:10:17]** The point is: nobody looks at a broken car and says, "Well, that's just the car's journey. The car lived a good life. We should accept the car's natural transition into rust." You call a mechanic. You are a car. Call a mechanic. Fund the mechanic.
