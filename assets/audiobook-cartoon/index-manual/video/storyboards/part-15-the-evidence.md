# Part 15 storyboard: "Why This Could Actually Work: The Evidence"

**Source:** `index-manual.qmd` "Why This Could Actually Work" and "The Evidence"; `knowledge/appendix/recovery-trial.qmd`.

**The numbers (from `_variables.yml`):**
- RECOVERY: 6 treatments and 48,000 patients (spoken, not parameters). Of the first six it tested, two worked (dexamethasone, tocilizumab) and four did not (hydroxychloroquine, lopinavir-ritonavir, azithromycin, convalescent plasma): on the board, bottles 1 and 5 get ticks.
- `recovery_trial_cost_per_patient` $500 against `traditional_phase3_cost_per_patient` $41,000: `recovery_trial_cost_reduction_factor` 82×. On a bar chart, the $41,000 bar is 82 of the $500 bars.
- `post_ww2_military_cut_pct` 87.6%: US military spending fell from `us_military_spending_1945_peak_annual_2024usd` $1.42 trillion (1945) to `us_military_spending_1947_annual_2024usd` $176 billion (1947), in 2024 dollars.
- `us_military_spending_current_vs_prewar_multiplier` 30.6×: `us_military_spending_2024_annual` $886 billion against `us_military_spending_1939_annual_2024usd` $29 billion (1939).
- Bar heights in bills, one bill = 1939: 1939 = 1, 1945 = 49, 1947 = 6.1, now = 30.6. One percent of now is a third of a bill.
- 65 years (spoken): the farewell address was 1961, so the counter ends on 2026.

**What the viewer must leave with:** it has been done: a trial ran 82 times cheaper in the middle of a panic, and America cut its military 87.6% in two years and got a boom, so 1% is nothing; Eisenhower said so, and everyone clapped and ignored him.
