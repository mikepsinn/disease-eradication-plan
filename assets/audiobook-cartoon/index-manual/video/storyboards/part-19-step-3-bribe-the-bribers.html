Lobbyists have clients, not beliefs, so you hire them away: the same salary, a better legacy, and an economy 1.43 times larger.

1920 × 1080 · 1:09.8 · 8 scenes · audio index-manual.cbaf659d.mp3, 43:10.9 to 44:20.7

Changes from v2

  • Wishonia's office, the cream paper diagrams and her corner (PiP) are gone. She hosts from her lectern in three host shots, with the Lobbyist as her guest: conversion-therapy (he slides her a bribe), lobbyists-have-clients (his briefcase opens on the empty BELIEFS slot) and it-is-arithmetic (the Calculator from Part 16 does not scream; iris out).
  • The therapist's office and its flood become a plate where the capitol is a slot machine: one war bill in the door, three missiles on the reels, a jackpot of 1,813 out of the dome, and the money buries the capitol.
  • The spreadsheet on the easel (row labels, a dial, a PHILANTHROPIST certificate) becomes a ledger page the camera travels down, two columns of three cells, one picture per phrase: the pay window, a police lineup between the arms dealer and the raisin baker, his headstone among orphans; then the same pay window with the seal flip, the cluster bomb tossed back over the column rule into the old pay window, a halo in the same lineup, and his statue with the brass plaque among the same children, now holding their parents' hands.
  • The two client cards and their ECONOMY bars become two little worlds in a starry sky: the current client's shrinks, the new client's grows to 1.43 times its size, and the Lobbyist dies on one and comes back on the other. The Calculator moves to Wishonia's lectern, where it lives since Part 16.
  • The therapist with a YOU badge is YOU, the bowler man of Parts 10, 12 and 17; the cards lose their CURRENT CLIENT and NEW CLIENT tabs (the portraits say it).
