# Part 5 storyboard: "The Budget Tower"

- **Source:** `knowledge/problem/nih-fails-2-institute-health.qmd`, "The Allocation Problem", the JAMA breakdown, from "A J.A.M.A. study" to "despite proven efficiency."
- **The numbers:** all from the JAMA study of N.I.H. spending, 2010 to 2019 (cited, not parameters), except where named.
  - Total: $247.3 billion.
  - Basic research: 84.9%, $209.9 billion.
  - Non-trial applied research: 11.8%, $29.3 billion.
  - The C.T.S.A. program: about $1 billion a year.
  - Observational cohorts: one costing $2.16 billion.
  - Clinical trials: `nih_clinical_trials_spending_pct` 3.3%, $8.1 billion. Of that: Phase 1 $1.5 billion (18.5%), Phase 2 $3.5 billion (43.2%), Phase 3 $2.6 billion (32.1%), unclassified $0.5 billion (6.2%).
  - The N.I.H. covers 3.7 to 4.3% of Phase 3 costs.
  - `recovery_trial_cost_reduction_factor`: pragmatic trials 82× cheaper per patient (`traditional_phase3_cost_per_patient` $41,000 against `recovery_trial_cost_per_patient` $500).
  - Derived for the tower: drawn to scale, if the building is 1,000 px tall, basic research is 849 px, applied 118 px, and trials 33 px.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** almost all of the N.I.H.'s money buys understanding; 3.3 cents of each dollar tests treatments, and even most of that leaves the last phase to drug companies.
- **Plates:**

  - `nih-tower`: the N.I.H. building stretched into a skyscraper on a plain field, its floors drawn to scale as the budget, a glass lift running down its face. A basement under the ground line; the drug company's tower across a back alley.
