# Paperback cartoon: kit moves to add

The collage moves the simplified paperback beat sheets ask for in their "Kit additions" that `index-manual/video/assets/js/collage.js` does not have, merged by what they do (the beat sheets name the same move many ways). Most-used first; the count is the beat-sheet parts that ask for it.

Left out because collage.js already does them: chart axes, bars that grow, shrink, sink below the baseline or run off the plate (`chart`, `bar`), dashed reference lines and dimension lines (`rule`, `line`), a line that draws itself and carries a rider on its tip (`line`), copies popping along a line or at listed points, such as headstones up a chart, ticks down a list or pins across a map (`chain`), piles, heaps, columns and bars of stacked copies, a pile that buries someone to the hand (`pile`, `stack`), rolling or ticking counters (`counter`), skeleton and see-through swaps on one cut-out, a group or a seeded random few (`xray`), copies walking a path or a loop in single file, lanes at different speeds (`march`), pictogram grids that grey out or swap one in N (`pictogram`), laps of a loop that speed up, grow, rise into a spiral or swap each lap, revolving doors included (`lap`), halves flying apart (`split`), state flips and costume changes (`swap`), swinging a needle, a branch, a lid or a tipping bar on its pivot (`hinge`), growing in steps with the feet planted, riding or following another cut-out (`move` with `scale` or `to`), camera pushes, pans and tilts up a tall plate (`camera`), plate washes and the lights going down (`wash`), and stamps, tags, banners, charring, flashes and shakes. Host props for `KIT.props` and things the beat sheets say are drawn in code are left out too.

- `flood` (16): a water level (or a flat colour) that rises across the plate or fills under a chart line or between two lines, lifting and floating cut-outs off as it reaches them, sinking one that lists under the waterline, then draining or swirling down a plughole (a heap of cut-outs circling like water down a drain); a puddle that spreads under a cut-out; a mass of dots that pours across and washes over a cut-out.
- `unroll` (16): a scroll, receipt, adding-machine tape, folded insert or accordion strip that unrolls or prints line by line along a path, down to the floor and along it, or off the frame and on, with a small cut-out or a bar beside each printed line; it keeps its length from one scene to the next, can jam, refuses to fold back, and can trail behind a moving cut-out; papers sliding along a glass tube while a ledger strip prints beside them.
- `strings` (15): a line that draws itself between two cut-outs and stays attached as they move: marionette strings from a hand or control bar that move a cut-out's wrists and head and go slack when cut; a cord or wire that plugs in with a spark, tugs the second cut-out, carries a running spark, or pulls tight and snaps (a chain breaking at a link and whipping away); a rope that winds round a cut-out and a prop; red string from pin to pin that sags and snaps; wires from one point to many that light at once; little flags each strung back to one point.
- `multiply` (13): a cut-out that shivers and splits into two copies on a flash, then four, eight and so on until a region is covered, or along branching lines in doubling rounds, each copy tapping the next two (the phone tree, the committees, the copier); copies that pop out of a tray faster and faster until they flow as a crowd; one cut-out splitting into N copies that walk off in turn; lines that grow out of a shape into a tree of nodes; dots on a globe that double each step.
- `axis` (10): a chart that changes under its marks: the axis relabelling while its bars grow so the picture holds, year labels sliding along the bottom (the curve moving one year per torn calendar page), paper extension sheets taped on above the chart so a bar or line can keep rising (and a line that drags its chart upward), a ruler's year marks extending on cue, axes and labels inking in round existing plate art.
- `redraw` (10): a cut-out rubbed out stroke by stroke by an eraser, or drawn in by a pencil along a path (a vine wrapping round other cut-outs, a chalk doodle growing a smokestack); a cut-out shown as a dotted outline that fills in, fades back to its outline, or flies a path; a cut-out replaced brick by brick by another.
- `scatter` (10): dots drawn in code on a chart, a few or thousands, each able to move, flow through a shape's outline or fizz off it, stand on a plank and tip with it; dots with error whiskers that draw out to both ends (and off the board); a forest plot; a floating range bar; average and median markers that slide on their own.
- `topple` (10): dominoes: a row or crowd of cut-outs that fall one into the next in turn, each with its own lean before it goes or a spin on its corner, with a counter that can run to thousands; pins knocked down one at a time; a whole cut-out falling flat from its base with a dust puff.
- `flow` (9): bills (or cars) carried along a pipe, tap or track at a set rate, thick or thin, with a meter that counts them, splitting at a valve, junction or switch in set shares (by count, 80/10/10, or by sliders), thinning stop by stop to drips; two streams where the bigger pulls the other's target across.
- `slice` (9): one cut-out cut into N strips or pieces that slide apart, walk off or fall as loose cut-outs (a bar sliced across, its top falling as missiles); a jagged crack that runs down through several charts and splits each one; a tear or unzip down a line that opens to show another cut-out inside; a chunk cut from the art that flies off; one copy tearing out of a repeating pattern.
- `stretch` (9): a cut-out lengthened along one axis over time (a beard or hair growing, a telescoping arm extending in steps, Grandma stretched), until it splits into two halves; a ruler losing length in steps or squeezing like an accordion; a plate that widens with its repeated pieces filling in; a cut-out leaning and stretching toward a point.
- `balance` (8): a balance scale or plank whose beam tips, eased, by the weight or height of what is in each pan, slides what is on it, springs flat when the weight lifts, flings the light pan's cut-out off the top of the frame, or breaks through the floor.
- `crowd` (8): thousands of tiny copies of a few cut-outs filling a field, a shape or a tiled pattern to the horizon, smaller with distance and fading to dots far away, each able to do one small move on its own beat (and a wave that ripples through them); a row of copies along a line to the horizon, each changeable on its own.
- `dial` (8): a needle or puck driven to a value over a set time, eased or with an overshoot, that can fall to zero, shake or fly off, and drives other cut-outs (how many in a row switch pose, a cut-out that stops when it does); a thermometer or progress bar whose column rises to a mark, falls short and drains, or overshoots and breaks its frame.
- `pass` (8): a cut-out handed from hand to hand along a row of cast members (sideways or up a staircase), shrinking a little or flipping a price tag at each hand, or a baton passed between runners as they meet.
- `queue` (8): copies of a cut-out in a line that each step up one place in turn; that file through a door or gate (most through one of two gates), fan out into several queues each to its own door, split at a fork onto two timelines at their own speeds, pile up nose to tail behind a gate, walk out of a bar one by one so it shrinks, or sit down all at once (and then age).
- `ratchet` (7): a wheel that turns one tooth per push or cue with a click and its pawl dropping in; pushed backward it holds and the pusher bounces off; it can drive a jack rising a notch per tooth, a value that only steps up, a line chart that can only climb, or a turnstile that clanks forward and refuses to turn back.
- `turn` (7): a cut-out turning to its other face with a squash, like a coin or card flipping on its vertical axis: a building rotating to show its other side, a card flipping again and again, a coin tossed to a chosen face, a plate object turning to face the other way on a turntable.
- `flap` (6): a split-flap board: letters, digits or whole rows that flip in one at a time (the tally board, a scoreboard, a fight card, an opinion board, a console flap sign).
- `inflate` (6): a cut-out swelling over time or in jerks with a squeak or rattle per step and its feet planted, then popping into pieces or into its charred version; swelling and shrinking on a beat (a breathing paper bag).
- `nest` (6): the camera pushing into a picture that holds a second plate (through a hinged lid, into a jar with the glass edge sliding past, into a pull-down chart that plays another plate), or zooming out through nested copies; a nesting doll that twists open and lifts a smaller one out, repeated.
- `peel` (6): a poster, sign face, sticker, chart strip or scratch panel that peels off to show what is under it (and falls, or stays), and a roller that paints it back.
- `ring` (6): copies of a cut-out placed round a circle, each able to turn its face to the centre, and the ring spun or drifting round a globe as one; a wheel with riders fixed to its rim, scaled per lap; a cut-out bent round a turning circle.
- `spray` (6): copies of a cut-out bursting out of a point and raining down: bills shot up out of the frame from a geyser or crater each time a clock strikes, a bundle bursting into single bills that land on many targets, jets of bills out of a pipe's holes, a payout of coins, water from a nozzle whose spread scales up.
- `wave` (6): one move fired down a row of cast members with a delay: treaties torn in turn, a pose passed along, eyes drooping with a rising Z, a row of heads gasping, a stamp applied square by square, faster and faster.
- `recolour` (5): a colour change over time on one cut-out only: blushing like a mood ring, going green then pink, Earth from blue to rust, a statue tinted bronze, a trophy gold; the plate shifting from sepia to full colour as the camera travels.
- `type` (5): text in code that types or writes itself (a terminal screen that types and redraws, a chalk equation that lights one symbol at a time, a number written digit by digit along a path, an acronym assembling from a phrase's capitals), swaps typeface in place, or whose letters drift loose and settle into a new word.
- `bounce` (4): cut-outs that hit an invisible dome and rebound with a shimmer; a spring punch out of a machine; a piece pushed down in one place popping up in another.
- `conveyor` (4): cut-outs riding a belt that are swapped at a station, go into a hopper or slot and come out of a door as something else, are fired back out across the frame, or jam at a fixed point.
- `follow` (4): the camera locked onto a moving cut-out along a path through the plate (a bill through the machine, a clock walking along a house, a receipt's end racing back in time), rather than keyed views.
- `grid` (4): a grid chart drawn in code from a few to millions of squares at plate scale, with labelled edges, whose cells light in a colour or fill in blocks, rows or all at once, with a camera pull-back that keeps a lit block's edge one plate unit wide.
- `pie` (4): a pie chart whose wedges light, move or fill with cut-outs one per word, also drawn on the Earth in the window.
- `pull` (4): one cut-out pulling another by a rope or chain: a hoist up a building floor by floor with a crowd heaving at the bottom, a pulley where one rises as the other falls, a tug of war with one side sliding.
- `rewind` (4): the plate's last moves played backward at speed with a projector flicker, then forward again.
- `split-screen` (4): the frame split down the middle with two halves playing at once, in mirror or one step apart; a comic-strip grid the camera visits panel by panel, each panel running its own small loop.
- `tilt` (4): the whole plate, or one plate piece, rotating about a corner, its base or a pivot, in steps or up to 180 degrees, with the cut-outs on it sliding down.
- `wobble` (4): a stack or tower that sways about its base and settles, or grows layer by layer wobblier until it falls apart from the bottom; a coin teetering on its edge and settling upright.
- `age` (3): a cut-out swapped through its life stages while it walks or sits, the new state following it (young to old to skeleton to dust; a sprout through its growth stages while scaling up).
- `crumble` (3): a cut-out that turns to dust, steam or pieces that blow off or drop away (many pieces falling off a sphere at once, a bar's top turning to steam), and the reverse.
- `overrun` (3): a sign, counter or stamp whose text grows longer than its sign or the frame (a threshold's digits that keep coming, a stamp impression that runs on past the frame for the camera to pan along).
- `rain` (3): falling streaks across the plate or inside a frame, drips, and cut-outs that fall from above and swap to other cut-outs as they land.
- `reels` (3): a slot machine's three windows (or three cut-outs, or a prize wheel) spinning and stopping on a set result.
- `reshape` (3): a drawn chart line whose points move after it is drawn: lifted from below like a tent pole, bent flat, or swung up about its end.
- `scramble` (3): many small copies converging on one point and scuffling, or stampeding and piling over each other along a path; a dust cloud with limbs, bricks and stars flying out that clears on the result.
- `sort` (3): cut-outs tumbling in a drum then flying to set places, shuffling into order by their numbers, or shot one each to a row of cast members.
- `stacked-bar` (3): a chart bar made of blocks that each grow on their own on top of the one below, each block a heap of cut-outs, in one or two columns.
- `flipbook` (2): pages riffling past at a set rate with a counter; a tear-off calendar that flips years while the plate's cut-outs change on each flip.
- `iris-shape` (2): an iris whose shape is a cut-out (the zero); a shutter flash with a round lens mask over the plate.
- `mirror` (2): a cut-out or half-plate that copies another's moves, flipped and a beat late, allowed to differ (the robot in the mirror, the lobby machine and its reflection).
- `onion-skin` (2): a half-transparent copy laid over a cut-out or the whole plate: two cut-outs sliding together to show they are identical; a tracing sheet that slides over the plate carrying its outlines, offset a hair.
- `pull-in` (2): a spiral that pulls cut-outs in, shrinking and spinning them (the black hole); a net that sweeps cut-outs off a path into a building.
- `smear` (2): a motion smear on a fast move: a whip pan along the plate, a prop passed back and forth until it blurs.
- `spin` (2): a cut-out rotating on the spot, faster and faster (the tail chase, the politician spinning at the signpost).
- `bubbles` (1): cards or syllables that drift off like soap bubbles and pop.
- `lights` (1): lit windows on a cut-out going dark one by one.
- `sign-swap` (1): a sign whose word swaps when two cut-outs come within a set distance of each other.
- `slowmo` (1): one cut-out at a quarter speed while one of its pieces moves at full speed.
- `sprite` (1): Wishonia's sprite placed on a plate, in a disguise (a fedora and moustache).
- `trapdoor` (1): a square that wears thin and opens into a hole a cut-out falls through.
- `unravel` (1): a cut-out's knit pieces pulled away along one thread into a growing yarn ball.
- `wrap` (1): a flat cut-out bending round the globe.
