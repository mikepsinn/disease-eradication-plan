# Part 6 storyboard: "How Your Researchers Get Paid"

- **Source:** `knowledge/solution/dih.qmd`, "How Your Researchers Get Paid", and its equation $S_i = v \cdot D_i$.
- **The numbers:**
  - The equation: $S_i = v \cdot D_i$. $S_i$ is the subsidy per patient enrolled in a trial for condition $i$; $D_i$ is the D.A.L.Y.s per patient for that condition (from the W.H.O.'s Global Burden of Disease data); $v$ is the value per D.A.L.Y., one constant, set as the annual budget divided by the global disease burden.
  - Spoken, not parameters: forty minutes (Wishonia) against seventy years (Earth's grant committees).
- **What the viewer must leave with:** each trial is paid per patient it enrolls, more for worse diseases; a trial that costs less than its subsidy gets run, one that costs more doesn't, and researchers keep what they save; funding continues only with results.
- **Note on the audio:** the narrator reads the equation's explanation with its symbols dropped ("Where is the subsidy...", "The only thing set centrally is which..."). The chalkboard shows $S_i$, $D_i$ and $v$ lighting up in each gap, so the picture says the missing words.
- **Plates:**
  - title card: "HOW YOUR RESEARCHERS GET PAID".
  - `carousel`: a fairground carousel on a plain field, four stops painted on its rim, a government office building beside it. After the crunch: its wreck, a small chalkboard, a brass balance scale and two trial doors with bulbs over them.
  - `flooded-lab`: a lab with a burst pipe in the ceiling.
  - Host scene (host-forty-minutes): Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the window.
- **Cast:** existing: `scientist`, `committee`, `chalkboard`, `ledger`, `balance-scale`, `trial-door`, `light-bulbs`, `patient` (also on a gurney), `bill-med`, `money-bundle`, `scissors`, `tumbleweed`, `sweeper`, `stopwatch`, `calendar`, `office-building`, `wrench`, `rosette` (the medal). - **Callbacks:** Part 5's bus-sized bundle and single coin are the two ends of the funding bar. Part 1's forty-minute rejection comes back as Wishonia's forty-minute discovery.
- **New art:**
  - `carousel`: a fairground carousel with painted horses; its rim panels take words in code; the same carousel crushed flat.
  - `plumber`: a 1950s plumber in overalls and a cap with a big wrench and a toolbox.

- voiceover: "The more patients who join your trial and the more severe the condition, the more funding you'll get. Patients vote with their enrollment. If trial cost exceeds the subsidy, nobody runs it, which is correct, because it means the health value doesn't justify the expense.
