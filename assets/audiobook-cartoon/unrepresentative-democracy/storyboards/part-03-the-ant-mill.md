# Part 3 storyboard: "The Death Spiral"

- **Source:** `knowledge/problem/unrepresentative-democracy.qmd`, "The Death Spiral: How Two Choices Kill 330 Million Options", and its ant-mill figure (the narrator reads its caption and credit).
- **The numbers:**
  - 330 million people (the narrator's; `us_population_2024` is 335 million). Two candidates. Derived: 2 out of 330 million is 1 in 165 million.
  - Surowiecki's four conditions (cited `surowiecki-2004`): diversity of opinion, independence of judgment, decentralization, a good aggregation mechanism. Democracy has three; it destroys independence.
  - Charles Mackay, 1841 (cited `mackay-madness-of-crowds`). 200 years of political science (the narrator's).
  - "Approximately nine people per state, seven of whom are angry" (the joke, not a statistic).
  - Ant mills: "sometimes hundreds of thousands strong" (cited `army-ant-death-spiral`). The circle takes four years (one election).
- **What the viewer must leave with:** a crowd is wise only when each person decides for themselves; two-party voting makes everyone copy everyone else, like blind ants following each other in a circle until they die.
- **Plates:**
  - `four-conditions`: a chalkboard on a plain field with four lines; a fish tank to one side.
  - `talent-pool`: a crowd of 330 million to the horizon, a claw machine standing over it, a ballot box in front.

  - Host: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.

- **New art:** a school of silver fish that moves as one, and the same fish wearing one tiny wristwatch; Charles Mackay in 1841 frock coat and side-whiskers, holding his book, and flattened with footprints; a fairground claw machine big enough to lift a man; an engraved army ant in thick round glasses, in walking poses, and its skeleton (one sheet).

- time: 08:39.3 to 09:35.7 (56.4 s)
- voiceover: "Army ants are nearly blind. They navigate by following pheromone trails laid by the ants ahead of them. When a foraging column gets separated from the main trail, the leading ants have nothing to follow, so they wander. But they're still laying pheromone as they walk. If the front of the column curves enough to cross its own trail, the leaders pick up that signal and follow it. The circle closes. Every ant is now both following and reinforcing the path that traps it. They march in a loop, sometimes hundreds of thousands strong, until they die of exhaustion. No individual ant is stupid.
