- **Source:** `knowledge/problem/unrepresentative-democracy.qmd`, its description, opening and "What Democracy Looks Like From Space" (the audio was read from an earlier draft: the narration script `unrepresentative-democracy.prepared.txt`, from the chapter title to "Naming is not your strength.").
- **The numbers:**
  - `military_to_government_clinical_trials_spending_ratio`: 604 (weapons spending for every 1 spent on testing which medicines work). Drawn as two bars, the weapons bar 604 times the trial bar's height.
  - `us_congress_member_count`: 535 members (the 535 Gandhis). "Within two years" is the narrator's, not a parameter.
  - Princeton (Gilens and Page, cited `princeton-oligarchy-study`, not parameters): 1,779 policy decisions over 20 years; correlation between ordinary voters' wishes and law, 0%; when economic elites want something it passes 78% of the time; when you want it, 30%, whether 0% or 100% of you want it.
  - `us_vote_decisive_probability`: 1 in 60 million. `us_vote_expected_value`: $0.000338 (the 1 in 60 million times federal spending per person, about $20,300).
  - The cost of voting, about $50 (the narrator's, not a parameter). Derived: you lose about $49.9997 per vote.
  - `us_total_lobbying_annual`: $4.4 billion a year on lobbying.
