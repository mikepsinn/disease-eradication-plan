# Part 4 storyboard: "The Math for the Mega-Corporation"

- **Source:** `knowledge/problem/fda-is-unsafe-and-ineffective.qmd`, sections "High Costs Kill Innovation, Reward Monopoly" and "Off-Patent Drugs and Rare Diseases: Mathematically Doomed".
- **The numbers:**
  - `pharma_drug_development_cost_current`: $2.6 billion to bring one drug to market, failures included.
  - `pharma_success_rate_current_pct`: 10% (nine candidates fail for each one that succeeds).
  - Rare diseases: 95% of diseases are rare and have zero treatments (cited, `95-pct-diseases-no-treatment`); about 10,000 patients each (the narrator's figure); $2.6 billion ÷ 10,000 = $260,000 per patient. `rare_diseases_count_global` is 7,000 diseases (not spoken).

  - For the chart: old drug revenue flat at about $300 million a year; option one drops to −$2.6 billion, then climbs at the same $300 million a year, so it is still $2.6 billion behind after any number of years.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** a company that already sells a drug for a disease loses $2.6 billion by making a better one and loses nothing by shelving it, and a disease with 10,000 patients would cost $260,000 a patient, so most diseases get no treatment at all.
