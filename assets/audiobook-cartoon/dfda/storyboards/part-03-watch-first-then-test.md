# Part 3 storyboard: "The Power of Real-World Evidence" and "The Two-Stage Pipeline"

- **Source:** `knowledge/solution/dfda.qmd`, "The Power of Real-World Evidence (Or: Spying on Sick People for a Good Cause)" (with its figure of paired observational and randomized estimates) and "The Two-Stage Pipeline: Watch First, Then Test".
- **The numbers:**
  - The meta-analysis (New England Journal of Medicine, cited, not a parameter): 19 treatments compared both ways; nearly identical effect sizes.
  - `dfda_observational_cost_per_patient` $0.1 a patient for Stage One.
  - The top 0.1% to 1% of signals go on to Stage Two (spoken, not a parameter).
  - `dfda_pragmatic_trial_cost_per_patient` $929 a patient for Stage Two; `traditional_phase3_cost_per_patient` $41,000; `dfda_trial_cost_reduction_factor` 44.1× (the narrator's "one forty-fourth the cost").
  - $57 million to test one drug the old way (cited `clinical-trial-cost-breakdown`, hardcoded in the chapter); "a million treatments" watched for pennies.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** watching real patients gets the same answers as costly trials, so watch everyone for about ten cents each, then run cheap randomized trials on only the best few signals.

  - `grandmas-day`: Grandma in her armchair on a plain field; beside her a line chart on aged paper, "ARTHRITIS PAIN", 30 days along the bottom, ON and OFF bands.
