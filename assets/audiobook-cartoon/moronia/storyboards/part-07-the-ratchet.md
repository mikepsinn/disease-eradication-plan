# Part 7 storyboard: "The Ratchet"

- **Source:** `knowledge/futures/moronia.qmd`, "The Ratchet", from its heading through "The ratchet had no reverse gear because building one was nobody's job."
- **The numbers:** none from `_variables.yml`. The story's numbers, as narrated:
  - Government's share of total output: about 10% at the start of two centuries of data, 50% when she found them. For the chart: one line over 200 years from 10% to 50%, rising in steps at each crisis.
  - Ten million papers to one contractor, against one paper from each of ten million taxpayers (the same ten million papers, seen from each end).
  - The cost of a program per person: less than the cost of a meal.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** government grows in crises and never shrinks back, because the few who gain from each program fight for it and the many who pay each lose too little to fight.
- **Plates:**
  - `the-pie`: a Moronian's kitchen table on a plain field, the giant brain in its suit from Part 1 looming over it; a line chart, "GOVERNMENT'S SHARE OF EVERYTHING PRODUCED", on the wall.
  - `the-ratchet`: a giant engraved ratchet wheel and pawl on a plain field, with the Capitol on the screw jack it turns; at its foot a balance scale, and a crowd of ten million (near ones people, far ones dots) stretching to the horizon.

## Scene 3 · ratchet-effect

- id: ratchet-effect
- act: The ratchet
- transition_in: cut
- cue: "A Moronian economist had described"
- time: 26:14.9 to 27:23.4 (68.6 s)
- voiceover: "A Moronian economist had described the mechanism precisely. He called it the ratchet effect. During every crisis (war, plague, financial panic) government expanded its powers, its budgets, and its control over individual decisions. When the crisis ended, the powers and budgets did not fully contract. They ratcheted. Each crisis left government slightly larger, slightly more intrusive, operating from a new baseline that became the floor for the next expansion. Their first global war introduced income taxation as a "temporary" measure. It became permanent. Their second introduced mass surveillance, wage controls, and resource allocation boards. The surveillance became permanent. The terrorism (manufactured by the bases, as I described) introduced warrantless monitoring, indefinite detention without trial, and a security apparatus that classified its own budget. All permanent. Each crisis was a one-way valve. Powers flowed in. They did not flow out."

## Scene 4 · olson-math

- id: olson-math
- act: Why it never reverses
- transition_in: same plate (the camera drops to the scale at the ratchet's foot)
- cue: "Another Moronian economist"
- time: 27:23.4 to 28:30.9 (67.5 s)
- voiceover: "Another Moronian economist, the Olson I referenced earlier, explained why the ratchet never reversed. The beneficiaries of each government program were concentrated and organized. The costs were spread across millions of taxpayers, each losing an amount too small to notice. Ten million papers to a specific contractor was worth lobbying for. One paper from each of ten million taxpayers was not worth resisting. So every program grew, every beneficiary lobbied for more, and the taxpayers never organized because the per-person cost of each individual program was less than the cost of a meal. By the time the aggregate cost was visible, the beneficiaries had become an industry with lobbyists who funded the committee members' reelection campaigns. Reversing a program was politically more expensive than expanding it. So they expanded. Always. All of them. In one direction. The ratchet had no reverse gear because building one was nobody's job."
- layer: B, plate the-ratchet
- bit: **The same ten million papers, seen from both ends: one bus-sized bundle lands in front of one contractor, and one paper floats off each of ten million heads, each worth less than a sandwich.** Contractor after contractor lines up, the ratchet clicks once per word, and the camera ends on its REVERSE GEAR slot, empty.
