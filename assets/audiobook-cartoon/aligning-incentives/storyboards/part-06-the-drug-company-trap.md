# Part 6 storyboard: "The Drug Company Trap"

- **Source:** `knowledge/solution/aligning-incentives.qmd`, "Pharmaceutical Companies: Converting Drug Dealers to Drug Curers", "Their Current Trap" and "The Math That Converts Them" (to "your department's coffee budget"); the cheaper trials are `knowledge/solution/dih.qmd`.
- **The numbers:**
  - `pharma_drug_development_cost_current` $2.6 billion per new drug; `pharma_success_rate_current_pct` 10%; `pharma_drug_revenue_average_current` $6.7 billion per success; 17 years to develop (prose).
  - `pharma_roi_current_system_pct`: 1.2%, Deloitte's 2022 low for the top 20 drug companies; 5.9% by 2024 (prose).
  - `dfda_trial_cost_reduction_factor` 44.1x cheaper; `traditional_phase3_cost_per_patient` $41,000 per participant; `dfda_trial_cost_reduction_pct` 97.7%.
  - A 50,000-participant trial: about $2 billion now, about $46 million decentralized (prose; 50,000 × $41,000 = $2.05 billion, 50,000 × $929 = $46 million). Drawn to scale, if the $2 billion bar is 900 px tall, the $46 million bar is 21 px.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** drug companies follow the money like a river runs downhill, their research is a bad bet at $2.6 billion a drug, and trials run through ordinary doctors' offices cost 44 times less, so the bet becomes small enough to make often.

  - `return-chart`: a line chart, "RETURN ON LATE-STAGE DRUGS", whose right end is a cliff edge.
