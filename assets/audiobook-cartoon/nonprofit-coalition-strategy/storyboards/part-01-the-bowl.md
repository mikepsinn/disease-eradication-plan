# Part 1 storyboard: "The Bowl"

- **Source:** the chapter opening of `knowledge/strategy/nonprofit-coalition-strategy.qmd` ("Why Every Nonprofit Should Support a 1% Treaty" up to "It requires looking behind you."), and `knowledge/appendix/parameters-and-calculations.qmd` for the numbers.
- **The numbers:**
  - `treaty_annual_funding`: $27.2 billion a year (1% of military spending).
  - `military_to_government_clinical_trials_spending_ratio`: 604. It is `global_military_spending_annual_2024` ($2.72 trillion) over `global_government_clinical_trials_spending_annual` ($4.5 billion).
  - `global_med_research_spending`: $67.5 billion a year (all medical research; the bowl).
  - For the chart: if the military bar is 900 px tall, medical research is 22 px and government trials are 1.5 px.
  - For the 1% corner (the narrator's pie): government trials are 0.6° of a pie of military plus trials; adding the 1% ($27.2 billion, to $31.7 billion) widens that slice to about 4.2°, seven times wider, while the military slice keeps 99% (`defense_sector_retention_pct`). On screen the corner is seven bowls' worth, and the banquet looks no shorter.
  - From the text, not variables: 80 years of watching; malaria kills 600,000 people a year and has a $7 billion nonprofit ecosystem; polio "nearly eradicated" for 25 years; cancer second leading cause of death.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** disease charities fight each other for a small and shrinking bowl, the war budget beside it is 604 times bigger, and moving 1% of it creates $27.2 billion a year so nobody has to fight.
- **Plates:**
  - `bowl-and-banquet`: a plain field; one dented dog bowl in front, a heap of thousands of skinny dogs round it, and behind them a banquet table running off the frame. It stands on end into a two-bar chart in the-604-bar, and its 1% corner is marked off in bigger-pie.
  - `titanic`: the tilting deck of a 1950s ocean liner on a flat sea, and the iceberg.
  - Host scenes (host-bakery, host-disorder, host-arithmetic): Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.
- **Cast:** existing: `germ`, `patient`, `patient-skeleton`, `bill-med`, `bill-war`, `money-bundle`, `money-hill`, `calendar`, `beard`, `banner`, `medal`, `trophy`, `price-tag`, `deckchair`, `mushroom`, `missile`, `calculator` (host prop), `mosquito`, `farm-stand` (the bakery), `economist` with `flip-board` (the consultant), `grandma` (the donor). New: `starving-dog`, `dog-bowl`, `banquet-table`, `ocean-liner`, `war-mountain`.
- **Callbacks:** episode 1, Part 1 (604-papers): the banquet standing on end climbs past the same clouds, warplane and pigeon as the 604 tower. The dogs, the bowl and the banquet return in every part of this chapter, and the one dog that almost turns round here is the one that finally does in Part 7.
- **New art:**
  - `starving-dog`: a sheet of skinny engraved mutts of every breed (a terrier, a basset, a greyhound, a poodle, a bulldog, a mongrel), each with a collar and a blank tag (cause names are code). Poses: snarling over a bowl, tug-of-war with a bill in its jaws, sitting and begging, in a necktie, in a bow tie, on its hind legs, ear cocked and head half-turned, turned all the way round, charred. One small copy for the heap. Later parts use the same sheet: a bucket in its jaws (Part 2), the bulldog lifting (Part 3), in blinkers (Part 4), knitting in a cardigan (Part 5), lying in bed (Part 6).
  - `dog-bowl`: a dented tin dog bowl with a few crumbs; also empty.
  - `banquet-table`: an endless Victorian banquet table under a white cloth, heaped with roasts, a suckling pig, cakes, fruit, and silver cloches (each lifts to show a plain `missile` on a platter). It can stand on end, and its near end can slide away along a dotted line.
  - `ocean-liner`: the deck of a 1950s ocean liner with a rail and funnels, tilting.
  - `war-mountain`: a snow-capped mountain built of war bills with a plain missile nose for its peak. Here it floats as the iceberg; in Part 4 it stands as the mountain.
- **Kit additions:**
  - `heap`: many copies of a small cut-out jostling in a pile, each on its own wobble (the dogs).
  - `stretch`: a cut-out lengthened along one axis over time (Grandma).
  - `stand-up`: a plate element hinging 90° from lying to standing, the camera tilting with it (the banquet into a bar).
