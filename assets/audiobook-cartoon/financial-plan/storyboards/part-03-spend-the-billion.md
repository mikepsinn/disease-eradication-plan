# Part 3 storyboard: "Pillar Two: The Spending Part" and "The Part That Makes It Cheap"

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/treaty-financial-plan.qmd`, "Pillar 2: The Spending Part" (its budget table and "The Part That Makes It Cheap"); the line items are in `knowledge/economics/campaign-budget.qmd`, and the referendum in `knowledge/strategy/global-referendum.qmd`.
- **The numbers:**
  - `treaty_campaign_total_cost`: $1 billion, cut three ways: `treaty_campaign_viral_referendum_base_case` $250 million (25%), `treaty_campaign_budget_lobbying` $650 million (65%), `treaty_campaign_budget_reserve` $100 million (10%). The strips are drawn to those widths.
  - `global_population_2024`: 8 billion people.
  - `three_point_five_percent_of_global_population`: 280 million votes. For the referral tree: doubling from one click reaches 280 million in 29 rounds (2^28 is 268 million).
  - Lobbying comparison (Part 2's figures, hard-coded in the chapter): pharma about $300 million a year, the military-industrial complex about $150 million a year.
  - Cost per vote, hard-coded in the chapter: $0.20 at the optimistic lower bound, base case $0.50 to $0.89, against $5 to $15 per voter through traditional channels. Drawn to scale: if the $15 bar is 900 px, the 20-cent bar is 12 px.
  - `chain_world_leader_count`: 195 countries; the campaign focuses on 20 of them.
  - Liechtenstein: 39,000 people (hard-coded in the chapter).
  - The timeline, hard-coded: 36 to 60 months optimistic, 120 or more months realistic. The landmine ban took 6 years (the narrator's figure; the Ottawa Treaty was signed in 1997).
- **What the viewer must leave with:** the $1 billion buys a referendum, lobbying and a reserve; votes cost cents because the referendum spreads person to person; and you only need 20 countries.
- **Plates:**

The camera travels over the globe's surface to a speck in the Alps (Liechtenstein) and to a field in Canada where the landmine negotiators sit, with a timeline in months along the bottom of the frame.
  - `the-billion-bill`: a plain field: one engraved banknote as wide as the frame, "$1 BILLION", ruled into three strips at 25%, 65% and 10%. Once it is cut, the same field holds the tree of clicking citizens, then the COST PER VOTE chart whose bars become the two buttons' stands.
  - Title card: "PILLAR TWO · THE SPENDING PART", with Part 1's column of bundles.
  - Host scene (host-open-question): Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the window.
- **Cast:** existing: `bundle-med`, `plinth`, `you`, `globe`, `mushroom`, `pointing-hand`, `magnifier`, `scissors`, `citizen`, `yes-button`, `laptop`, `robot`, `super-pac`, `campaign-poster`, `factory` (the brick factory that flips over to a lab), `lab`, `pharma-proud` (from Part 2), `factory-proud`, `coin`, `bill-plain`, `pallet`, `price-tag`, `pencil`, `display-case`, `landmine`, `calendar`, `war-table`, `politician` (in its tints, as the negotiators), `case-file`.
