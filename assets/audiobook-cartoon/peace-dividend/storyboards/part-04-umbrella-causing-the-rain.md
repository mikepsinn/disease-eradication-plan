## Scene 4 · bases-make-terror

- cue: "Here's why the elasticity"
- time: 11:45.1 to 12:58.2 (73.2 s)
- voiceover: "Here's why the elasticity is probably better than one point zero: military spending doesn't just respond to threats; it generates them. Robert Pape at the University of Chicago found that ninety-five percent of suicide terrorist attacks from nineteen eighty to two thousand and three were responses to foreign military occupation, not ideology. Half the attackers had no religious motivation at all (the leading group was atheist Marxists). Your eight trillion dollar War on Terror took terrorist attacks from about one thousand per year to nearly seventeen thousand by twenty fourteen. Bases provoke resistance, resistance justifies bigger budgets, bigger budgets fund more bases. It's a feedback loop, and feedback loops don't scale linearly. Cut one percent of the bases, you remove one percent of the provocation, which removes more than one percent of the terrorism, which removes the justification for more than one percent of next year's budget. The elasticity isn't one point zero because you're not just reducing spending; you're interrupting the cycle that manufactures the thing you're spending on. It's like discovering that your umbrella was causing the rain. (See the full data on occupation and terrorism.)."
