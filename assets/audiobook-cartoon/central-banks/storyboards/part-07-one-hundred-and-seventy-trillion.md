# Part 7 storyboard: "One Hundred and Seventy Trillion"

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/central-banks.qmd`, "The Receipt", from its footnote to "that is a different chapter."
- **The numbers:**
  - Footnote (cited, not parameters): costs are US military spending in 2024 dollars from CRS (FY2011 estimates), except the post-9/11 wars' $8 trillion from Brown University. Higher counts: up to 85 million for the Second World War, up to 4.7 million post-9/11; the less conservative total exceeds 110 million (against the receipt's 97 million).
  - `global_military_spending_annual_2024` = $2.72 trillion a year, up 9.4% on the year before (SIPRI, cited).
  - `military_to_government_clinical_trials_spending_ratio` = 604: one year of military spending is 604 years of government clinical trials. So trials get `global_government_clinical_trials_spending_annual` = $4.5 billion a year.
  - The Fed has run for 111 years (the narrator's figure; the book now says 113).
  - `cumulative_military_spending_fed_era` = $170 trillion since 1913, central estimate, range $150 to $190 trillion. The stack (cited): 1988 to 2024 $65 to $72 trillion; Cold War (1946 to 1987) $50 to $70 trillion; the Second World War $23 trillion; the First $7 trillion; interwar $3 to $5 trillion. All warfare before 1913 too: about $180 trillion. Violence's yearly cost (IEP): $20 trillion.
  - Derived: $170 trillion / $4.5 billion = about 37,800 years, the narrator's "nearly thirty-eight thousand years". Trials have run about 80 years, so the tower is about 475 times the whole history of trials.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** the killing budget never stops, and since 1913 it would have paid for 38,000 years of the trials humans have been running for 80.
- **Plates:**
  - `the-receipt` (Part 6): the receipt's foot, close, and the mountain behind.
  - `the-tower`: the weapons factory on a plain field, a conveyor of war bills running into its door from the Fed building; beside it a bar chart whose bars stack into a tower of strapped money bundles rising past the clouds; then the same tower lying flat along a timeline, 38,000 YEARS.
  - `science-fair`: a Wishonian science fair: two tables, two ribbons.
  - Host scenes: none in this part.
- **Cast:** existing: `receipt`, `magnifier`, `helmet-skeletons` (Part 6), `factory-pay`, `fed-crank-up`, `conveyor`, `banner`, `calendar`, `money-bundle` (as war-bill bundles), `dotted-stack`, `accountant`, `coin`, `cloud`, `goose`, `scientist`, `rosette`, `plinth`, `wishonian` (Part 1, the eleven-year-old and a friend), `ledger-computer` (Part 1, a tiny copy on her board). - **Callbacks:** Part 6's receipt and mountain. Part 1's eleven-year-old Wishonian and her ring of computers, now at the fair.
- **New art:**
  - `fungus`: a fat engraved toadstool with little arms, and the abacus it slides beads on.
  - The plates. All numbers, era labels, the footnote text and the timeline are code; the ribbons are `rosette` recoloured.
