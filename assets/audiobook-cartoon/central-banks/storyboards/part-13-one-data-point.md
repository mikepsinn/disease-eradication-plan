# Part 13 storyboard: "The One Data Point"

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/central-banks.qmd`, "The One Data Point" (citations `bis-deflation-historical-2015`, `borio-costs-deflation-2015`).
- **The numbers:** none are `_variables.yml` parameters; the chapter cites them inline.
  - 1870 to 1896: the longest US deflation, 26 years. Prices fell about 2% a year; real output grew 2 to 3% a year; real wages rose.
  - The Panic of 1873 (one bad year inside the 26).
  - The BIS study (Borio et al. 2015): 140 years, 38 economies. Full sample: a "weak" link; without the Great Depression: no significant link. The Great Depression: GDP fell 30%.
  - Goods-price deflation: no link to slower growth. Property-price deflation: linked (2008).
  - For the picture: the economics "Nobel" is the Sveriges Riksbank Prize, endowed by Sweden's central bank in 1968 (a fact, not spoken; it is the gag on the medal).
- **What the viewer must leave with:** the only evidence that falling prices are dangerous is the Great Depression, whose falling prices were caused by failing banks, and with that one point removed the link disappears.
- **Plates:**
  - `one-dot`: an inked chart on aged paper, "FALLING PRICES vs. GROWTH", with one big dot at the bottom, then the street of universities behind it. The cathedral lands on the same dot at the end.
  - `long-deflation`: a two-line chart, 1870 to 1896: "PRICES" sloping down and "OUTPUT" climbing, with a railroad laid along the OUTPUT line.
  - `plank`: a scatter chart whose trend line is a wooden plank on a pivot, thousands of small dots standing on it, the one big dot holding its end down, the BIS tower at the right.
  - Host scenes: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.
- **Cast:** existing: `economist` (with Part 12's storybook and nightcap), `fed-crank-up` / `fed-crank-down`, `bank-legs`, `schoolhouse` (as the colleges, copied down a street; the plaque is code), `price-tag`, `goods`, `cloud`, `light-bulbs`, `phone-pole`, `medal`, `house`, `report-card`, `magnifier`, `scissors`; from Part 10: `locomotive`. New: `bis-tower` (the round Bank for International Settlements tower in Basel with a smug face; a video-game health bar over it in code), `cathedral` (a Gothic cathedral, PERMANENT INFLATION over its door, which can topple). The one data point is a chart mark drawn big and heavy in code, the title card's full stop, not a cut-out.
- **Callbacks:** the title card's heavy full stop is the dot that holds the plank down and the dot the cathedral stands on. Part 12's economist, still flattened and holding his storybook, peels himself off the pavement in scene 2. The bank buildings that fall here are the same `bank-legs` that fail by the thousand in Part 14.
- **New art:** the three plates above and the two new cut-outs. Chart titles, axes, the plaques and stamps are code.
- **Kit additions:**
  - `scatter`: a few thousand dots placed on a chart, able to stand on a plank and tip with it.
  - A pivot: a plank that tilts by an angle with a weight (the big dot) at one end and springs flat when the weight lifts.
  - Dominoes: a row of cut-outs that fall in turn.

## Scene 6 · boss-fight

- voiceover: "The Bank for International Settlements (the central bank of central banks, which is a real thing that exists, like a boss fight at the end of a video game about money) tested this across one hundred and forty years and thirty-eight economies. Their full-sample result: the link between deflation and slower growth is "weak." Their result when you remove the Great Depression: no statistically significant link at all. The Great Depression was so extreme (G.D.P. fell thirty percent) that it acts as a statistical leverage point, pulling the result negative against thousands of observations showing the opposite.
