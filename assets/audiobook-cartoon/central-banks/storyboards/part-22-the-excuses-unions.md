# Part 22 storyboard: "But It Wasn't the Gold Standard, It Was..." (the excuses, and unions)

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/central-banks.qmd`, "But It Wasn't the Gold Standard, It Was..." (first excuse). The narration is `central-banks.prepared.txt`.
- **The numbers:** none from `_variables.yml`. The narrator's figures, hardcoded in the chapter:
  - Six alternative explanations; the wage gap opened in 1971 (the gold window closed).
  - Union membership peaked in 1954 and fell through the late 1950s and the 1960s, while wages grew fastest: 20 years of opposite lines (1954 to about 1971 in the chart).
  - A 3% raise that took 15 years of organizing, erased by one Tuesday afternoon's printing.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** the unions did not cause the wage gap; pulling the gold anchor in 1971 let inflation eat their strike funds, so unions and wages drained out together.
- **Plates:**
  - Host: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.
  - `lineup`: a police lineup wall on aged paper whose height lines are years, 1950 at the left to 2010 at the right (it extends far left to 1602 in Part 23). Inked across it, the PRODUCTIVITY and PAY lines run together until 1971, where PAY goes flat. On the floor at 1971 lies a worker's skeleton, still clutching his paycheck: the body. Parts 22 to 24 share it.
  - `bathtub`: a clawfoot bathtub on a plain field, its water marked WAGES, its plug a gold bar on a chain; the Federal Reserve press beside it, the little brick mill behind it.
- **Cast:** existing: `fed-crank-up` / `fed-crank-down`, `fat-cat-catch`, `skeleton` and `paycheck` (the body at 1971), `sign-board` (the picket sign), `laptop` (the computers, two copies), `moth`, `calendar`. From earlier parts: `economist` with his magnifier, `friedman` (Part 5), `share-certificate` (Part 16). New: `union-man`, `nixon`, `bathtub`, `mill`, `moving-van`, `reagan`, `ape`, `pistol`.
- **Callbacks:** the six suspects stay in the lineup through Parts 23 and 24; the skeleton at 1971 is the body every suspect arrives late for. Nixon's gold plug comes back as the bottle's cork in Part 24.
- **New art:**
  - `union-man`: a 1950s union worker in a flat cap, rolled sleeves and overalls, hugging a STRIKE FUND jar of bills; his beard as a piece that grows to the floor.
  - `nixon`: Richard Nixon caricatured, jowls, ski-slope nose, five-o'clock shadow, both hands in V-signs; one hand that grips the gold plug (the plug is the cork in Part 24, the gold in Part 30).
  - `bathtub`: a clawfoot bathtub, a drain that swirls, the plug as a gold bar on a chain.
  - `mill`: a plain 1950s brick factory with one smokestack; standing up on stick legs with a suitcase (also Parts 24 and 25).
  - `moving-van`, `reagan`, `ape` (two engraved apes in a tree, one hugging the whole trunk, for Part 24): suspects that stand in the lineup here.
  - `pistol`: a plain revolver (also Part 27's duel).

  - `drain`: the water and whatever floats in it swirl down the plughole.

## Scene 4 · the-tub

- id: the-tub
- act: Unions
- transition_in: cut
- cue: "And here is the part your economists"
- time: 65:00.2 to 66:02.3 (62.1 s)
- voiceover: "And here is the part your economists leave out: unions didn't collapse on their own. The gold anchor had kept inflation low, which meant union strike funds held their value, which meant unions could actually afford to strike. Remove the anchor, and inflation eats the strike fund. Now your union is a man holding an empty gun. Meanwhile, floating exchange rates made it trivially easy to move the whole factory to a country where workers accept less. Worse, corporations no longer need to negotiate at all: they borrow cheap money from the Fed, buy back their own stock, and watch their share price rise. The Fed prints money on a Tuesday afternoon and erases a three percent raise that took fifteen years of organizing. Why negotiate with a union when the printer is faster, quieter, and doesn't picket? Unions didn't drag wages down. The gold anchor was pulled, and both unions and wages drained out of the same bathtub. Your economists are blaming the water for leaving."
