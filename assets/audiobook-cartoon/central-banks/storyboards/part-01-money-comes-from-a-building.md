# Part 1 storyboard: "Money Comes From a Building and They Use It to Kill People"

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/central-banks.qmd`, from the top through "But the most interesting thing about your money is not what it buys."
- **The numbers:**
  - 97 million dead in wars the money funded: the narrator's number (the audio's six-war receipt total, Part 6). The book now prints `war_deaths_since_1900` = 310 million here; the screen uses the narrator's 97,000,000.
  - Dollar purchasing power kept since 1913: 4% (cited in the chapter, not a parameter). For the bar: a 1913 dollar drawn full size, today's dollar 4% of its area.
  - Wishonia: rocks for about 400 years, then 200 years of arguing and three currency collapses; the inventor was 11 (story numbers, not parameters).
  - Blockchain: 2009. The ape NFT: $69 million (as narrated; not a parameter).
- **What the viewer must leave with:** money is made in a building by people nobody elected, it reaches friends and weapons before it reaches you, and an honest replacement already exists.
- **Plates:**
  - `the-building`: the Fed as its building on a plain street, cranking itself; a road runs right from its paper tray past the fat cat's door and the weapons factory, a long way on to YOU. Under the street, a cutaway of the ground: a mine on the left, a vault on the right.
  - `the-ledger`: Wishonia's field of boulders, and beside it a ring of computers on a plain floor. The same ring is Earth's in monkey-jpgs (a plaque swaps WISHONIA for EARTH).
  - Host scenes: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.
- **Cast:** existing: `fed-crank-up` / `fed-crank-down`, `factory-pay`, `fat-cat`, `you`, `bill-war`, `bill-plain`, `loaf`, `headstone`, `tv-set`, `ballot-box`, `cobweb`, `moth`, `rosette`, `sheets`, `auctioneer`, `miner`, `vault-door`, `pea-bulb`, `rock-gray`, `crowd`, `mushroom`. New: `ledger-computer`, `wishonian`, `ape-picture`, `gold-bar`.
- **Callbacks:** the Fed building is episode 1's press (Part 2's MONETARY POLICY plate), here cranking itself. The `moth` flies out of the empty ballot box, as from every empty thing in episode 1.
- **New art:**
  - `ledger-computer`: a 1950s cabinet computer with a screen showing a little ledger page, drawn once and copied round a ring; one state blushing.
  - `wishonian`: little Wishonians in her colours, pushing a boulder, arguing, handing a rock under a table; and the eleven-year-old with her science-fair trifold.
  - `ape-picture`: an ape portrait in a gilt frame, and the same frame empty (Part 2 hangs a dog in a hat in it).
  - `gold-bar`: a gleaming nugget and the same gold as a bar.
  - The two plates. Signs, numbers, ledger lines and the words on the screens are code.
- **Kit additions:**
  - `chart`: a bar drawn as a bill that shrinks to a set share of its area (4%).
  - A ring: copies of one cut-out placed round a circle, each able to turn its face to the centre.
