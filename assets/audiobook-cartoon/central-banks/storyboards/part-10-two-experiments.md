# Part 10 storyboard: "Two Experiments"

- **Source:** `knowledge/economics/central-banks.qmd`, "What He Killed", from "From 1948 to 1973" to the end of the section.
- **The numbers** (cited in the chapter, not parameters):
  - 1948 to 1973: the typical family's income grew 3% a year, doubling about once a generation. Real GDP grew 4.2% a year in the 1950s and 4.5% in the 1960s. Homeownership rose from 44% to over 62%. Inequality (the Gini coefficient) fell for two decades.
  - The classical gold standard, 1870 to 1914: real GDP per person grew 3.8% a year in the 1880s. From 1880 to 1896 prices fell 30% and real GDP rose 85%.
  - Bretton Woods: 27 years (1944 to 1971). After Nixon, median income growth fell from nearly 3% to 0.6% a year: an 80% fall.
  - Both gold periods: about double the median income growth of the fiat periods after them.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** both times money was tied to gold, ordinary families got steadily richer, and when the tie was cut their gains mostly stopped.
- **Plates:**
  - `income-street`: a line chart on aged paper, "TYPICAL FAMILY INCOME", 1948 to 1973, its line a street that a 1950s world-of-tomorrow neighbourhood grows along.
  - `the-anchor`: the Fed building chained to a gold bar, beside a plain factory (no missiles: `factory`'s lab side) with a pay window.
  - `two-experiments`: one sheet of aged paper with two charts the camera travels between: a two-line chart, "PRICES" and "OUTPUT", 1880 to 1896, with inventions along the rising line; and a two-bar chart, "MEDIAN INCOME GROWTH PER YEAR", BEFORE 1971 and AFTER.
  - Host scenes: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass.
- **Cast:** existing: `family`, `house-of-tomorrow`, `flying-car`, `motorcar`, `grandma`, `paycheck` (the pay packet), `fat-cat`, `top-hat`, `fed-crank-up`, `gold-bar` (Part 1), `chain-link` (Part 4), `factory` (flipped to its lab side, no missiles), `economist`, `display-case`, `telephone`, `light-bulb`, `loaf`, `price-tag`, `pointing-hand`, `nixon` (Part 8), `scissors`, `you`, `moth`. - **New art:**
  - `locomotive`: an 1880s steam engine.
