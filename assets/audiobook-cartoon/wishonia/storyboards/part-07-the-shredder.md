# Part 7 storyboard: "The Shredder"

- **Source:** `knowledge/futures/wishonia.qmd`, "The Peace Dividend Economy", from "The money doesn't trickle down" to "the most expensive free money your species has ever discovered." (narration script lines 130 to 143; the audio was read from a longer draft of this passage than the chapter has now). It links `knowledge/solution/automated-revenue-service.qmd` for the IRS.
- **The numbers:**
  - `us_gov_waste_tax_compliance`: $546 billion a year on collecting taxes.
  - "seven point nine billion hours of lost productivity": the narrator's figure (cited in the draft, not a parameter); on screen, 7.9 BILLION HOURS.
  - `us_gov_waste_regulatory_red_tape`: $580 billion a year on regulatory compliance.
  - Derived: $546 billion + $580 billion = $1.126 trillion, the narrator's "over a trillion dollars per year on the process of permission".
  - `us_gov_waste_total`: $4.98 trillion in the book now; the narrator says "four point nine trillion dollars", and the screen shows $4.9 TRILLION.
  - `us_gov_waste_pct_gdp`: 17.3% of GDP; the narrator says "seventeen percent", and the pie's slice is 17%.
  - "The entire economy of Japan": the narrator's comparison (Japan's GDP is roughly $4 trillion a year), not a parameter.
  - "Tens of thousands" of IRS employees: the narrator's words (the chapter now says 80,000); no number on screen.
- **What the viewer must leave with:** one automatic tax pays one automatic income, and that ends a waste the size of Japan's economy that exists only to process paperwork.
- **Plates:**
  - `ubi-street`: a street of houses of tomorrow on aged paper, a shop at the far end, a pedestal at its head where the champagne tower stands first and the smart contract after. The street doubles as the racetrack.
  - `permission-row`: a desk on a plain floor, a row of four government offices with one queue, a trapdoor across the floor, and at the right end the giant shredder with the US GDP pie on an easel.
  - Host: Wishonia at her console on the observation deck, Earth in the glass, the Calculator on the console.
- **Cast:** existing: `fat-cat`, `top-hat`, `bill-plain`, `citizen`, `sign`, `pentagon`, `house-of-tomorrow`, `calendar`, `claim-form`, `balance-scale`, `auditor` (the bureaucrat with the magnifying glass), `hungry-man`, `cash-register`, `scissors`, `tax-office` (the IRS building, and recoloured as the four offices), `accountant`, `tabulator`, `wall-clock`, `lawyer`, `ladder`, `calculator` (host prop), `desk`, `you`, `birthday-cake` (from Part 2, candles off), `trapdoor`, `pie-chart`, `country-maps` (with Japan added). New: `champagne-tower`, `smart-contract`, `shredder`.
- **Callbacks:**
  - Episode 1, Part 7 (museum-of-knowing): the DEPARTMENT OF DEFENSE sign that peeled to DEPARTMENT OF WAR; the narrator names it, and the TRICKLE-DOWN sign peels the same way.
  - Part 3 (nine-am): the bill through YOU's mail slot came from this machine; Part 3's tiny tax office is the IRS building.
  - Set up for Part 8: the IRS building's trapdoor, which the Department of Education follows; the smart contract also pays out the health credit.
- **New art:**
  - `champagne-tower`: a pyramid of coupe glasses with green bills sloshing in them.
  - `smart-contract`: a glass-cased clockwork machine on a pedestal, a scroll of code turning inside it, a chute out of the front and a pipe into the back. It does the same job on every street.
  - `shredder`: a giant office paper shredder with a slot on top and a little office box built onto its side; drawn once and scaled up for the bigger ones.
  - `country-maps`: add Japan, with Mount Fuji, a factory and a bullet train on it.
  - The `ubi-street` and `permission-row` plates. The office signs, the confetti and the price boards are code.
- **Kit additions:**
  - `walk` on a closed path: the slivers of each purchase returning up the pipe to the smart contract.
  - `zoom out` through nested copies (the shredder eating the shredder).
  - `peel`: the sign peel (also in Part 6).
