Why Your Trials Cost a Tesla Per Person.

Traditional Phase three trials cost forty-one thousand dollars per patient. A Tesla. Per person. To find out if a pill works. The active ingredient often costs pennies to synthesize. The paperwork weighs more than the patient.

Then the Oxford RECOVERY trial proved you can do the same thing for five hundred dollars. During a pandemic. While panicking. They tested COVID treatments on forty thousand plus patients by:

Using existing hospital staff (doctors who were already treating patients and could presumably continue doing so while also writing things down), collecting data electronically (instead of sacrificing forests to the paperwork gods), and asking "does the patient live or die?" instead of "did they complete form twenty-seven B in triplicate?" 

The U.S.-based ADAPTABLE trial later confirmed this wasn't a pandemic fluke: nine hundred and twenty-nine dollars per patient in routine American healthcare settings, no emergency, no N.H.S. A systematic review of sixty-four pragmatic trials found a median cost of ninety-seven dollars per patient. The low cost is a property of the design, not the crisis.

Scale that globally: up to forty-four point one times cost reduction. Same data quality. Your governments spend four point five billion dollars per year on clinical trials. Industry adds another fifty-five point five billion dollars, mostly on drugs that are already profitable.
Scale RECOVERY-style pragmatic trials across the full sixty billion dollars market and you save up to fifty-eight point six billion dollars annually. That's before counting the lives. I'm saving those for dramatic effect.
