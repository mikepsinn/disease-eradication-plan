Financial Plan

Invest one billion dollars. Pass a treaty. Manage twenty-seven point two billion dollars a year forever. Eighty percent cures diseases, ten percent pays investors, ten percent keeps politicians obedient. Three pillars, no committees, no generosity required.

Most financial plans are complicated because the writers are confused or lying. This one is complicated because you're convincing one hundred and ninety-five countries to voluntarily stop buying so many weapons while paying your investors forever.

The architecture is embarrassingly simple. Raise one billion dollars. Spend it to pass the treaty. Manage twenty-seven point two billion dollars plus annually forever. Three things. That's the whole financial plan. The rest of this chapter is just explaining why your species needs four thousand words to understand three things.

Pillar One: The Money Part (Raise one billion dollars).

The instrument is a VICTORY Incentive Alignment Bond, which is a piece of paper that says "give us money now, get much more money later, forever." On Wishonia, this would be called "fraud." On Earth, it's called "finance." The difference is collateral.

The bonds return two hundred and seventy-two percent annually. This looks like a typo. It's arithmetic: two point seven two billion dollars divided by one billion dollars. Senior debt structure, meaning the fund pays bondholders before anyone else, because investors don't care about your moral framework until after they've been paid.
They receive ten percent of all treaty inflows (two point seven two billion dollars a year), collateralized against the entire military budget of the planet. Every Ponzi scheme in history has promised returns like this. The difference is that Bernie Madoff's collateral was a spreadsheet he made up.
Your collateral is twenty-seven point two billion dollars plus in annual treaty revenue, which, unlike Bernie's spreadsheet, actually exists.

You raise one billion dollars in twelve to twenty-four months. Donations would require generosity. Bonds require greed. Greed is faster. This is the single most important insight about your species and I've been trying to communicate it for four thousand two hundred and ninety-seven years.

Why one billion dollars? Your opposition spends four hundred and fifty million dollars a year to keep things exactly as terrible as they are (pharma lobbying at roughly three hundred million dollars a year plus the military-industrial complex at roughly one hundred and fifty million dollars a year). A subscription to suffering. You raise one billion dollars ONCE to pass a treaty that generates twenty-seven point two billion dollars a year FOREVER.
Over a ten-year campaign, opposition spends roughly four point five billion dollars to your one billion dollars. But their four hundred and fifty million dollars a year is spread across thousands of contracts and dozens of incumbents. Your one billion dollars is aimed at a single treaty. Concentrated offense beats diffuse defense.
This is military strategy, which is ironic, and also the only kind of strategy your species respects.

Nobody gets paid unless it works. Lawyers get success-based compensation. Team compensation is mostly equity. This is how you know it's not a government program.

The Seed phase requires two hundred and fifty to four hundred million dollars for the platform, policy frameworks, and pilots, with a milestone of fifty million verified participants and a difficulty level of "Convince a few billionaires".

The Series A phase requires five hundred million to one billion dollars to scale to G Seven nations, with a milestone of three point five percent global participation (two hundred and eighty million people) and a difficulty level of "Convince several countries".

The Growth phase requires five hundred million to one point one billion dollars for the ratification push in major powers, with a milestone of the first one billion dollar disbursement and a difficulty level of "Convince the countries with nukes".

Ranges reflect scenario planning (optimistic to pessimistic). The one billion dollar target is the base case; upper bounds cover contingencies because convincing nuclear powers to behave rationally occasionally requires overtime.

Pillar two: The Spending Part (Spend one billion dollars).

The one-time activation energy to convince eight billion people to marginally reduce their commitment to mutual annihilation. Detailed line items are available.

The Global Referendum category takes two hundred and fifty million dollars for the viral referral system, two hundred and eighty million votes, and platform development.

The Political Lobbying category takes six hundred and fifty million dollars for A.I.-targeted campaigns (U.S./E.U./G Twenty), Super PACs, and Military-Industrial Complex, or M.I.C., conversion. This outspends pharma plus the M.I.C.

The Reserve Fund category takes one hundred million dollars for the post-passage transition and a contingency buffer.

The Part That Makes It Cheap.

Viral referendum mechanics bring the cost down to twenty cents per vote at the optimistic lower bound (base case fifty cents to eighty-nine cents), compared to five to fifteen dollars per voter through traditional channels.

Getting someone to click "I prefer not dying" costs less than getting someone to click "I prefer Pepsi." You also focus on twenty high-impact countries, not all one hundred and ninety-five. You don't need Liechtenstein's permission. Liechtenstein has thirty-nine thousand people. That's a concert venue, not a geopolitical obstacle.

Timeline is thirty-six to sixty months (optimistic) to one hundred and twenty plus months (realistic, given that you're humans). The landmine ban took six years, and that was just agreeing not to hide explosives in the ground, which you'd think wouldn't require a treaty at all.

You're betting on viral referendum mechanics and existing disarmament frameworks. Also on the assumption that your species wants to survive, which, based on my four thousand two hundred and ninety-seven years of observation, remains an open question.

Pillar three: The Forever Part (Manage twenty-seven point two billion dollars or more per year).

Once the treaty passes, twenty-seven point two billion dollars flows annually from "how to kill people" budgets into "how to stop people dying" budgets. This is the part where your financial plan stops being a financial plan and starts being a money volcano that erupts on schedule, forever.

One hundred plus nations contribute one percent of military budgets. Not because they've had a moral awakening.
Because the Incentive Alignment Bond mechanism makes compliance self-enforcing: politicians in non-compliant countries watch their Public Good Scores drop, voters abandon them, and compliance becomes the career-optimal choice.
You don't need politicians to be good. You just need them to be selfish in the right direction.

How the Money Gets Spent: eighty, ten, ten.

The allocation is automatic. No committees. No debates. No one arguing for six months about font choices on the grant application. Just math, executing silently, like a competent employee (something most of your governments have never experienced).

Eighty percent of the allocation, or twenty-one point eight billion dollars, goes to Clinical Trials and Platform for patient subsidies, research, and the platform.

Ten percent, totaling two point seven two billion dollars, is for VICTORY Bond Returns for perpetual investor payments.

The final ten percent, another two point seven two billion dollars, goes to I.A.B. Political Incentives as rewards for supporting legislators.

The twenty percent for bonds and political incentives is untouchable. Smart contracts distribute both automatically. No human hands involved, which, given your track record with money, is a feature. On Wishonia, we learned four thousand two hundred and ninety-seven years ago that the best way to handle money is to never let a person touch it.

Your species hasn't learned this yet. Your species has a thing called an "accountant," which is a person you pay to watch your money, who sometimes takes your money, so you hire a second person called an "auditor" to watch the first person, and then the auditor takes your money.

The smart contract skips all of this.

The remaining eighty percent (twenty-one point eight billion dollars per year) funds clinical trials and research infrastructure. Wishocracy governs the allocation.

Dynamic Patient Subsidies.

Instead of paying researchers to write about diseases, you pay patients to participate in curing them. Paying the people who are actually sick. What a concept.

Patients organize to grow the treasury, because participating in their own survival is, for once, financially rewarded.

Researchers compete to attract patients instead of competing to write persuasive grant applications, which is a different skill from doing science, the same way writing a restaurant review is a different skill from cooking.

Insurance companies save money because trials are cheaper than decades of chronic suffering. Compare that to N.I.H. grants: six months of proposal writing, committees judging the writing, universities skimming forty percent overhead, and then maybe some science.

The Whole Thing on One Line.

Invest one billion dollars. Get two point seven two billion dollars per year. Forever. Your species spent roughly five billion dollars on a particle accelerator to find a boson. This costs less and finds a cure for dying.
