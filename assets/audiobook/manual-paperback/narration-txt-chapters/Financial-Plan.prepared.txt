Financial Plan

Most financial plans are complicated because the writers are confused or lying. This one has three steps. Raise one billion dollars (using greed, not generosity, because greed is faster). Spend it to pass the treaty. Manage twenty-seven point two billion dollars a year forever, with eighty percent curing diseases, ten percent paying investors, and ten percent keeping politicians obedient. The corruption is capped at twenty percent and fully transparent, which already makes it the most honest government program in your country's history.

The architecture is embarrassingly simple. Raise one billion dollars. Spend it to pass the treaty. Manage twenty-seven point two billion dollars or more annually forever. Three things. Pillar One: The Money Part (Raise one billion dollars).

The instrument is a VICTORY Incentive Alignment Bond, a perpetual contractual claim on a fixed share of treaty inflows: give us money now, get much more money later, for as long as the treaty survives.

On Wishonia, this would still be called "fraud." On Earth, it's a contingent revenue share backed by the treaty's future cash flows if the treaty passes.

The bonds return two hundred and seventy-two percent annually. This looks like a typo. It's arithmetic: two point seven two billion dollars divided by one billion dollars. This is not ordinary term debt with a maturity date; it is a treaty-level revenue share that contractually earmarks ten percent of all treaty inflows (two point seven two billion dollars per year) to bondholders for as long as the treaty exists.

That investor tranche is fixed by the treaty and the fund's governing rules, not by annual managerial discretion, because investors don't care about your moral framework until after they've been paid. Every Ponzi scheme in history has promised returns like this.

The difference is that Bernie Madoff's repayment story was a spreadsheet he made up. Yours is a treaty that, if passed, creates twenty-seven point two billion dollars or more in annual public funding and routes a fixed share of it to investors.

You raise one billion dollars in twelve to twenty-four months. Donations would require generosity. Bonds require greed. Greed is faster. 

Why one billion dollars? Your opposition spends four hundred and fifty million dollars per year to keep things exactly as terrible as they are (pharma lobbying at roughly three hundred million dollars per year plus the military-industrial complex at roughly one hundred and fifty million dollars per year). A subscription to suffering. You raise one billion dollars ONCE to pass a treaty that generates twenty-seven point two billion dollars per year FOREVER.

Over a ten-year campaign, opposition spends roughly four point five billion dollars to your one billion dollars. But their four hundred and fifty million dollars per year is spread across thousands of contracts and dozens of incumbents. Your one billion dollars is aimed at a single treaty. Concentrated offense beats diffuse defense.

This is military strategy, which is ironic, and also the only kind of strategy your species respects.

Nobody gets the upside unless it works. Lawyers can take success-based compensation. Team compensation is modest cash plus mostly equity. This is how you know it's not a government program.

The Seed phase is two hundred and fifty to four hundred million dollars for the platform, policy frameworks, and pilots, reaching fifty million verified participants with a difficulty level of "Convince a few billionaires".

The Series A phase is five hundred million to one billion dollars to scale to G Seven nations, with a milestone of three point five percent global participation (two hundred and eighty million people) and a difficulty level of "Convince several countries".

The Growth phase is five hundred million to one point one billion dollars for a ratification push in major powers, reaching the first one billion dollar disbursement with a difficulty level of "Convince the countries with nukes".

Ranges reflect scenario planning (optimistic to pessimistic). The one billion dollar target is the base case; upper bounds cover contingencies because convincing nuclear powers to behave rationally occasionally requires overtime.

The Part That Makes It Cheap.

Viral referendum mechanics bring the cost down to twenty cents per vote at the optimistic lower bound (base case fifty to eighty-nine cents), compared to five to fifteen dollars per voter through traditional channels.
Getting someone to click "I prefer not dying" costs less than getting someone to click "I prefer Pepsi." You also focus on twenty high-impact countries, not all one hundred and ninety-five. You don't need Liechtenstein's permission. Liechtenstein has thirty-nine thousand people. That's a concert venue, not a geopolitical obstacle.

Timeline is thirty-six to sixty months (optimistic) to one hundred and twenty or more months (realistic, given that you're humans). The landmine ban took six years, and that was just agreeing not to hide explosives in the ground, which you'd think wouldn't require a treaty at all.
You're betting on viral referendum mechanics and existing disarmament frameworks. Also on the assumption that your species wants to survive, which, based on my observation, remains an open question.

How the Money Gets Spent: eighty, ten, ten.

The allocation is fixed by the treaty and implemented through the fund's governing documents.
Victory Corporation (your S.P.V., owned by the D.I.H. Foundation) administers the treasury rails and investor distributions, while the political-incentive allocation funds legally separate scoring, electoral, and post-office entities described in the Legal Framework.
No committees. No debates. No one arguing for six months about font choices on the grant application. Just math, executing on schedule, like a competent employee (something most of your governments have never experienced).

Eighty percent, or twenty-one point eight billion dollars, is allocated to Clinical Trials and Platform for patient subsidies, research, and the platform.

Ten percent, or two point seven two billion dollars, goes to Victory Bond Returns for perpetual investor payments.

Ten percent, or two point seven two billion dollars, is dedicated to I.A.B. (Incentive Alignment Bond) political incentives as rewards for supporting legislators.

The twenty percent for bonds and political incentives is fixed by the same treaty and fund rules and cannot be reallocated by management because somebody had a better idea after lunch. Where automation is legally and operationally practical, treasury rules can execute the distributions mechanically.
Where the real world insists on banks, custodians, and auditors, the same formula still applies. Humans may maintain the payment rails. They do not get to improvise the percentages. On your planet, that already counts as a miracle.

The remaining eighty percent (twenty-one point eight billion dollars per year) funds clinical trials and research infrastructure. Patients direct trial-participation spending by joining studies. Wishocracy governs infrastructure and other shared public-good allocations.

Dynamic Patient Subsidies.

Instead of paying researchers to write about diseases, you pay patients to participate in curing them. Paying the people who are actually sick. What a concept.

Patients organize to grow the treasury, because participating in their own survival is, for once, financially rewarded.

Researchers and biotech companies compete to attract patients instead of competing to write persuasive grant applications, which is a different skill from doing science, the same way writing a restaurant review is a different skill from cooking.

Insurance companies save money because trials are cheaper than decades of chronic suffering. Compare that to N.I.H. grants: six months of proposal writing, committees judging the writing, universities skimming forty percent overhead, and then maybe some science.

The Whole Thing on One Line.

Invest one billion dollars. Get two point seven two billion dollars a year. Forever. Your species spent roughly five billion dollars on a particle accelerator to find a boson. This costs less and finds a cure for dying.
