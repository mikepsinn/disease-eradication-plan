*Phase 0: Collecting Humans Before You Have Anything to Offer Them*

Here's the problem. You want to end war and disease. You don't have a nonprofit, registered securities, or a bank account. What you do have is an argument and the audacity to think that might be enough.

This is Phase 0. The goal isn't money (you can't legally accept it yet). The goal is building a database of every human who agrees that dying from preventable causes is a strange thing to tolerate. You're collecting proof of demand before you have a product. It's a Kickstarter where the reward is "possibly not dying."

## The Bootstrapping Problem

Before you can accept donations, you need 501(c)(3) status. Before you can sell bonds, you need SEC registration. Before you can do either, you need a bank account. It's bureaucratic nesting dolls, each one requiring the previous one to already exist.

Humanity has solved this exact problem the same way every time: you collect people first, then point at the crowd when institutions ask "but who wants this?" Abolition, suffrage, civil rights. Someone wrote down names. The names became bargaining power. The bargaining power became policy. It's almost like democracy works when someone remembers to use it.

## Three Types of Allies

People who want to help fall into three categories, each requiring a slightly different dialect of persuasion.

### Investors

People who hear "272% annual return backed by health savings" and immediately open a spreadsheet.
They're not heartless.
They just process moral arguments faster when those arguments come with compound interest.
They'll wait if the math is good.
They will not wait if the math is vibes.

### Advocates

People motivated by the mission.
They share things on social media, write to politicians, volunteer time, and donate money they probably shouldn't.
Every movement in history was built by people like this.
They're the reason the spreadsheet people eventually have something to invest in.

Give them specific actions they can take right now.
Telling someone "care about this" without giving them something to do is how you manufacture apathy.

### Institutional Partners

Organizations (nonprofits, research institutions, health agencies) that already work on pieces of this problem.
They have infrastructure, credibility, and donor bases.
You have a framework that could make their existing work more effective.
This is a trade, not a favor.

Institutions are territorial in the way that dogs are territorial, except dogs eventually stop barking.

## Phase 0 Targets

By the end of Phase 0, you want:

- **100,000+ registered supporters** across all three categories
- **1,000+ qualified investors** with stated investment ranges
- **100+ institutional partners** with expressed interest
- **$1B+ in stated investment demand** (registered, not committed)

This database is your ammunition for everything that follows:

- 501(c)(3) applications ("here are our supporters")
- SEC registration ("here is market demand")
- Political meetings ("here are your constituents")
- Platform launch ("here are your users")

Bureaucrats don't respond to arguments. They respond to evidence that other people already responded to arguments.

## Getting Started

1. **Build three registration forms** (investors, advocates, partners). Takes twenty minutes.
2. **Put up a website.** The forms and the argument. It does not need to be pretty. Movements are not judged by their CSS.
3. **Start telling people.** Social media, Reddit, forums, conferences, your dentist.
4. **Send monthly updates.** Real progress, real numbers, real timeline. "3,247 registered investors representing $180M in stated interest" beats "the movement is growing!" because adults prefer arithmetic to enthusiasm.

The military-industrial complex has a 75-year head start, unlimited budgets, and thousands of lobbyists. You have a spreadsheet and an argument. Historically, that's been enough.
